Progression Gate Design: What Clears a Level?

Each level = Deep Dive (recognise) + Play-List Challenges (embody). What does "done" look like?

Four Approaches

A

Count-Based: "Complete X challenges at this layer"

Simple. Fixed number per layer (e.g., 5 Screen challenges = Level 1 cleared). Easy to understand, easy to track.

Pros

  • Clear, predictable
  • Easy to build progress bar for
  • Works for gamification (5/5 challenges done)

Cons

  • Doesn't measure QUALITY of engagement
  • Someone could rush through 5 easy Screen challenges without growth
  • Feels like homework, not a journey
B

Essence-Zone: "Hit the diagonal X times at this layer"

Must complete challenges where scary ≥ 7 AND wahoo ≥ 7 (the essence zone). This means you're doing challenges that genuinely scare you AND genuinely excite you. That's the Groan Zone diagonal from the framework.

Pros

  • Measures real growth, not just activity
  • Directly tied to the framework (Groan Zone = where growth happens)
  • Can't game it — you'd have to actually push your edge
  • The scoring already exists (calculateEssenceZone)

Cons

  • Harder to explain to users
  • Self-reported scores could be inflated
  • Early users might not hit essence zone at Screen level (too new to the concept)
C

Hybrid: "Complete X challenges, including Y in the essence zone"

E.g., "Complete 5 Screen challenges, at least 2 must be in the essence zone." Gets volume AND quality. Allows warm-up challenges before pushing the edge.

Pros

  • Best of both worlds
  • Allows gradual progression within a layer
  • Progress bar shows both metrics
  • Warm-up → edge → mastery arc within each level

Cons

  • More complex to communicate
  • Two numbers to track per level
D

Narrative: "The 4 R's within each level"

Each level has its own 4 R's arc: Recognise (deep dive) → Release (first few challenges, getting comfortable) → Rewire (essence zone challenges, pushing the edge) → Reconnect (reflection + voice check-in confirms shift). The gate isn't a number — it's completing the arc.

Pros

  • Tells a story within each level
  • Matches the framework's 4 R's methodology
  • Each challenge phase has different energy (release = volume, rewire = edge)
  • Reconnect phase = confirmation the shift happened

Cons

  • Harder to quantify for a progress bar
  • More complex to implement
  • Could feel slow for users who want clear metrics

Option D Expanded: The 4 R's Arc Within Each Level

Example: Level 1 (Identity / Screen Layer)

Recognise
Complete Shadow Work deep dive. Meet your essence archetype. See the protective pattern.
Gate: Deep dive complete
Release
3 Screen layer challenges. Any scary/wahoo score. Just start moving. Build the habit of showing up as yourself online.
Gate: 3 challenges completed
Rewire
2 Screen layer challenges in the ESSENCE ZONE (scary ≥ 7, wahoo ≥ 7). Push the edge. Do the one that scares AND excites you.
Gate: 2 essence zone challenges
Reconnect
Voice check-in: "Is my essence voice louder than my protective voice at this layer?" If yes → level cleared. If no → more Rewire challenges.
Gate: Voice check-in confirms shift
Total per level: 1 deep dive + 3 warm-up challenges + 2 essence zone challenges + 1 voice confirmation = ~6 challenges + 1 deep dive per level.
At 1-2 challenges per week, each level takes roughly 3-4 weeks. Full 6-level journey = ~6 months. That feels right for genuine nervous system rewiring.
