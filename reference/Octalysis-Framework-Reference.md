# The Octalysis Framework
## A Complete Reference Guide to Gamification & Human Motivation

---

## Overview

The Octalysis Framework was created by **Yu-kai Chou**, recognized as one of the world's leading experts in gamification. After 20+ years of research and work with companies like Google, Tesla, LEGO, and Stanford University, Chou developed this framework to explain why games are so engaging and how those principles can be applied to any product, service, or experience.

The framework is built on a fundamental insight: **all human motivation can be traced back to 8 Core Drives**. Everything we do is driven by one or more of these motivations. When none of these drives are present, there is no motivation, and no behavior happens.

## Core Drive 1: Epic Meaning & Calling

### Definition
The drive where users believe they are doing something greater than themselves or are "chosen" to do something.

### Psychology
Humans have a deep need to feel their lives have meaning and purpose. When people believe they're contributing to something bigger, they become highly motivated and often volunteer their time without financial compensation.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Narrative** | A compelling story that gives context and meaning | Games with rich backstories explaining why the player matters |
| **Humanity Hero** | Making users feel they're helping humanity | Wikipedia editors contributing to "sum of all human knowledge" |
| **Elitism** | Creating pride in being part of an exclusive group | Apple's "Think Different" positioning |
| **Beginner's Luck** | Making users feel specially chosen from the start | Getting a rare item early in a game |
| **Free Lunch** | Feeling lucky or blessed to receive something | Unexpected bonuses that feel like destiny |
| **Destiny Child** | Users feel they were "meant" to find this | "You were destined for this journey" messaging |
| **Co-Creator** | Users help build/shape the product itself | Early beta testers who feel ownership of direction |

### Real-World Applications
- **Wikipedia**: Contributors believe they're building humanity's knowledge base
- **Apple**: "Think Different" made customers feel part of a movement
- **TOMS Shoes**: "One for One" gives purchases deeper meaning
- **Environmental movements**: "Save the planet" framing

### Design Questions
- Why should the user care beyond personal benefit?
- What larger mission can the user contribute to?
- How can we make users feel "chosen" or special?
- What story gives their actions meaning?

## Core Drive 2: Development & Accomplishment

### Definition
The internal drive to make progress, develop skills, and overcome challenges.

### Psychology
This is the most common drive used in gamification. Humans feel satisfaction when they grow, improve, and master challenges — but only when the challenge is meaningful. Points, badges, and leaderboards only work when they represent genuine progress.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Points** | Numerical representation of progress | XP, score, credits |
| **Badges/Achievements** | Visual symbols of accomplishment | Trophies, medals, certifications |
| **Leaderboards** | Rankings showing relative position | Top 10 lists, percentile rankings |
| **Progress Bars** | Visual display of completion status | Profile completeness, level progress |
| **Fixed Action Rewards** | Known rewards for specific actions | "Complete X to earn Y" |
| **Status Points** | Points that determine rank/tier | VIP levels, loyalty tiers |
| **Win States** | Clear definitions of success | Completion screens, victory messages |
| **Boss Fights** | Major challenges that test skills | Final exams, certification tests |
| **Certificates** | Formal recognition of achievement | Completion certificates, credentials |
| **High Fives** | Social acknowledgment of progress | Congratulations messages |
| **Crowning** | Public recognition of achievement | "Player of the Week" features |
| **Anticipation Parade** | Building excitement before reward | Countdown to unlock, drumroll effects |
| **Aura Effect** | Visual indicators of status | Glowing avatars, special badges visible to others |
| **Step-by-Step Tutorial** | Guided early wins | Onboarding that ensures first success |
| **Desert Oasis** | Easy win after difficult stretch | Recovery points in challenging sequences |

### Real-World Applications
- **LinkedIn**: Profile completeness progress bar
- **Duolingo**: Streaks, XP, skill trees
- **Fitness apps**: Achievement badges for milestones
- **Video games**: Level systems, skill trees

### Design Questions
- What skills should users develop?
- How do we show progress visually?
- What are meaningful milestones?
- How do we ensure challenges are achievable but not trivial?
- What does "mastery" look like?

### Warning
Points and badges without meaningful challenge create hollow motivation. The achievement must represent real growth or users will see through it.

## Core Drive 3: Empowerment of Creativity & Feedback

### Definition
The drive to express creativity, experiment with combinations, and receive meaningful feedback.

### Psychology
When users have tools to be creative and can immediately see the results of their creativity, they become deeply engaged. This is "evergreen" motivation — users never tire of expressing themselves and seeing outcomes. It's the drive behind why people spend hours in sandbox games or creative tools.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Blank Fills** | Templates users customize | Mad Libs, fill-in-the-blank prompts |
| **Milestone Unlock** | New tools/options earned through progress | Unlocking new building materials in Minecraft |
| **Boosters** | Power-ups that amplify creative output | Special abilities, enhanced tools |
| **General's Carrot** | Resources to deploy strategically | Budget allocation, point spending |
| **Choice Perception** | Multiple paths to same goal | Different strategies that all work |
| **Plant Picker** | Choosing from meaningful options | Character classes, loadout selection |
| **Instant Feedback** | Immediate response to actions | Real-time previews, live results |
| **Evergreen Mechanics** | Infinitely replayable creativity | Sandbox modes, open-ended building |
| **Real-Time Control** | Direct manipulation of elements | Drag-and-drop interfaces |
| **Chain Combos** | Combining elements for enhanced results | Skill combinations, recipe systems |
| **Poison Picker** | Choices with trade-offs | Risk/reward decisions |
| **Voluntary Autonomy** | Freedom to choose own path | Open-world exploration |

### Real-World Applications
- **Minecraft**: Endless building possibilities
- **Canva**: Creative templates with infinite customization
- **LEGO**: Open-ended construction
- **Music production software**: Unlimited creative expression

### Design Questions
- What tools can users combine creatively?
- How quickly do users see results of their choices?
- What meaningful choices can users make?
- How do we enable "infinite replayability"?
- What unique creations can users produce?

### Key Insight
This is the strongest "evergreen" motivator. Unlike achievements (which end) or curiosity (which resolves), creativity is infinite. Products that enable meaningful creative expression have the highest long-term engagement.

## Core Drive 4: Ownership & Possession

### Definition
The drive to own things, improve what we own, and protect our possessions.

### Psychology
When users feel ownership over something, they become motivated to improve and protect it. This applies to virtual items, customized experiences, and even invested time. The "IKEA Effect" shows people value things more when they helped create them.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Virtual Goods** | Digital items users own | Skins, avatars, collectibles |
| **Build From Scratch** | Creating something from nothing | Character creation, profile building |
| **Collection Sets** | Groups of items to collect | Pokemon, trading cards |
| **Avatar** | Personalized representation | Custom characters, profile photos |
| **Learning Curve** | Investment creates attachment | Time spent = ownership felt |
| **Alfred Effect** | System learns user preferences | Personalized recommendations |
| **Recruitment** | Bringing others creates investment | Referral programs |
| **Monitoring** | Tracking investments over time | Portfolio dashboards, progress logs |
| **Exchangeable Points** | Currency that can be spent | Credits, tokens, coins |
| **Protection** | Safeguarding what's been built | Save features, backup systems |

### Real-World Applications
- **Spotify**: "Your" playlists, personalized recommendations
- **LinkedIn**: "Your" profile and network
- **Gaming**: Customizable characters, inventories
- **Loyalty programs**: Points accumulation

### Design Questions
- What can users "own" in your system?
- How can users customize/personalize their experience?
- What happens when users invest time?
- How do we make users protective of their progress?
- What collections can users build?

### Key Insight
Ownership doesn't require physical goods. Users feel ownership over:
- Data they've contributed
- Time they've invested
- Customizations they've made
- Relationships they've built
- Progress they've achieved

## Core Drive 5: Social Influence & Relatedness

### Definition
The drive related to social elements: mentorship, acceptance, competition, companionship, and envy.

### Psychology
Humans are fundamentally social creatures. We're motivated by what others think, do, and have. This includes positive drives (wanting to connect, help, belong) and comparative drives (wanting to be better than, have what others have).

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Mentorship** | Guiding/being guided by others | Coach features, buddy systems |
| **Social Prod** | Notifications about others' actions | "Your friend just completed..." |
| **Friending** | Building connections | Add friend, follow features |
| **Social Treasure** | Gifts only receivable from others | Referral bonuses, friend gifts |
| **Group Quests** | Challenges requiring collaboration | Team challenges, community goals |
| **Touting** | Sharing achievements publicly | Share to social media |
| **Brag Button** | Easy way to showcase accomplishments | "Share your results" prompts |
| **Water Cooler** | Social gathering spaces | Forums, comment sections, community hubs |
| **Conformity Anchor** | Showing what others are doing | "Most popular choice" indicators |
| **Thank-You Economy** | Gratitude-based interactions | Like buttons, appreciation features |
| **Rivalry** | Competitive relationships | Head-to-head challenges |
| **Social Prods** | Gentle nudges from peers | Invitations, challenges from friends |

### Real-World Applications
- **Facebook/Instagram**: Entire platforms built on social influence
- **Fitness apps**: Competing with friends, sharing achievements
- **Online reviews**: Social proof driving decisions
- **Workplace**: Recognition programs, team challenges

### Design Questions
- How can users connect with each other?
- What can users do together that they can't alone?
- How do we enable healthy competition?
- What social proof can influence decisions?
- How can users help each other?

### Warning
Social features can backfire if they create negative comparisons, toxic competition, or social anxiety. Design for connection, not just comparison.

## Core Drive 6: Scarcity & Impatience

### Definition
The drive of wanting something because it's rare, exclusive, or immediately unattainable.

### Psychology
We want what we can't have. Limited availability increases perceived value. This drive creates urgency and desire, but can also create frustration if overused.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Appointment Dynamics** | Specific times to take action | "Happy hour" bonuses, daily login rewards |
| **Fixed Intervals** | Rewards at predictable times | Daily rewards, weekly events |
| **Magnetic Caps** | Limits that make things valuable | "Only 100 spots available" |
| **Dangling** | Showing something just out of reach | Locked features visible but unavailable |
| **Prize Pacing** | Spreading rewards over time | Drip content, sequential unlocks |
| **Options Pacing** | Gradually revealing choices | New features unlocked at levels |
| **Patient Feedback** | Delayed gratification | Results revealed after waiting period |
| **Torture Breaks** | Forced pauses in action | Energy systems, cooldown timers |
| **Moats** | Barriers that create exclusivity | Application processes, qualification requirements |
| **Last Mile Drive** | Almost there momentum | "Just 1 more to complete set" |
| **Countdown Timer** | Visual urgency | "Offer expires in..." |
| **Contextual Scarcity** | Temporary availability | Seasonal items, limited editions |

### Real-World Applications
- **Flash sales**: Limited time offers
- **Exclusive memberships**: Waiting lists, application processes
- **Gaming**: Energy systems, daily limits
- **Product launches**: Limited first-run quantities

### Design Questions
- What could be limited (time, quantity, access)?
- How do we create anticipation?
- What "earned access" could users work toward?
- When does scarcity add value vs. frustrate?

### Warning
**This is a "Black Hat" drive.** It creates urgency and desire but can feel manipulative. Overuse leads to:
- User frustration
- Perception of manipulation
- Disengagement when urgency fatigue sets in

Use sparingly and ethically.

## Core Drive 7: Unpredictability & Curiosity

### Definition
The drive of wanting to know what happens next or discover what's unknown.

### Psychology
The human brain is wired to seek patterns and resolve uncertainty. When outcomes are variable, we remain engaged trying to understand and predict. This is why gambling, mystery boxes, and cliffhangers are so compelling.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Glowing Choice** | Highlighting unknown options | Mysterious unopened items |
| **Mystery Box** | Random rewards | Loot boxes, surprise packages |
| **Random Rewards** | Variable reinforcement | Slot machine mechanics |
| **Easter Eggs** | Hidden surprises | Secret features, hidden content |
| **Rolling Rewards** | Cumulative random bonuses | Streak bonuses with variable multipliers |
| **Evolved UI** | Interface that reveals over time | Hidden features that appear with use |
| **Sudden Rewards** | Unexpected bonuses | Surprise drops, random gifts |
| **Oracle Effect** | Predictions/forecasts | "Based on your data, you might..." |
| **Mini Quests** | Small unexpected challenges | Daily surprises, pop-up events |
| **Visual Storytelling** | Narrative reveals | Story progression, lore discovery |
| **Obvious Wonder** | Clearly interesting unknowns | "What's behind that door?" |

### Real-World Applications
- **Netflix**: "What happens next" in series
- **Social media**: Variable reward of new content in feeds
- **Gaming**: Loot systems, random drops
- **Email**: The curiosity of "new message"

### Design Questions
- What mysteries can users discover?
- Where can we add positive surprises?
- How do we create "what happens next" anticipation?
- What hidden depth exists to uncover?

### Warning
**This is a "Black Hat" drive.** While curiosity is natural, variable reward systems (especially combined with scarcity) can become addictive. The gambling industry exploits this drive. Use ethically:
- Focus on discovery and learning, not gambling mechanics
- Ensure users feel satisfied, not manipulated
- Avoid exploitative random reward systems

## Core Drive 8: Loss & Avoidance

### Definition
The drive to avoid negative outcomes, loss of progress, or missed opportunities.

### Psychology
Psychologically, humans are more motivated to avoid losing something than to gain something of equal value (loss aversion). Fear of missing out (FOMO), protecting progress, and avoiding negative consequences are powerful motivators.

### Game Techniques

| Technique | Description | Example |
|-----------|-------------|---------|
| **Rightful Heritage** | Feeling of deserving something | "You've earned this — don't lose it" |
| **Evanescence Opportunity** | Disappearing chances | "This offer expires forever in..." |
| **Status Quo Sloth** | Resistance to change/effort | Default options, easy paths |
| **Visual Grave** | Showing what was lost | Displaying expired opportunities |
| **Sunk Cost Prison** | Investment prevents quitting | "You've come this far..." |
| **Progress Loss** | Potential to lose achievements | Streak loss, rank demotion |
| **Countdown Timers** | Urgency through time pressure | "Only 2 hours left" |
| **FOMO Punch** | Fear of missing out | "Everyone else is doing this" |
| **Weep Tune** | Emotional consequence display | Sad music when losing |
| **Scarlet Letter** | Public display of failure | Visible to others when you fail |

### Real-World Applications
- **Snapchat**: Streak loss fear
- **Insurance**: Fear of loss motivates purchase
- **Subscriptions**: "Don't lose your benefits"
- **Gaming**: Lives, health bars, fail states

### Design Questions
- What could users lose that they care about?
- How do we make stakes feel real without causing anxiety?
- What opportunities could expire?
- Where does "protecting progress" make sense?

### Warning
**This is the strongest "Black Hat" drive.** It's highly effective but:
- Can create anxiety and stress
- Can feel manipulative and exploitative
- Can damage brand trust
- Can lead to unhealthy behaviors

Use with extreme caution, especially in wellness/health contexts. Frame positively when possible: "Keep your streak going!" vs "Don't lose your streak!"

## White Hat vs. Black Hat Motivation

### The Spectrum

Octalysis divides the 8 Core Drives into two categories based on how they make users feel:

```
         WHITE HAT (Top of Octagon)
    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
    │  CD1: Epic Meaning & Calling    │  ← Empowering
    │  CD2: Development & Accomplish. │  ← Empowering
    │  CD3: Creativity & Feedback     │  ← Empowering
    └─────────────────────────────────┘
    
         BLACK HAT (Bottom of Octagon)
    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
    │  CD6: Scarcity & Impatience     │  ← Urgent/Obsessive
    │  CD7: Unpredictability & Curios.│  ← Urgent/Obsessive
    │  CD8: Loss & Avoidance          │  ← Urgent/Obsessive
    └─────────────────────────────────┘
    
         NEUTRAL (Middle)
    ┌─────────────────────────────────┐
    │  CD4: Ownership & Possession    │  ← Can be either
    │  CD5: Social Influence          │  ← Can be either
    └─────────────────────────────────┘
```

### White Hat Motivation

**Characteristics:**
- Makes users feel powerful, fulfilled, satisfied
- Users engage because they WANT to
- Creates long-term loyalty and positive brand association
- Users feel good about themselves after engaging

**Best for:**
- Building lasting relationships
- Products where user wellbeing matters
- Creating brand advocates
- Sustainable engagement

**Limitation:**
- Doesn't create urgency
- Users may delay action because there's no pressure

### Black Hat Motivation

**Characteristics:**
- Creates urgency, obsession, addiction
- Users engage because they feel they HAVE to
- Can drive immediate action
- Users may feel uneasy or manipulated afterward

**Best for:**
- Driving immediate conversion
- Creating urgency when needed
- Short-term behavioral pushes

**Limitation:**
- Doesn't create long-term loyalty
- Can damage brand trust
- Can cause user burnout
- Ethical concerns

### The Ideal Approach

**Use White Hat to attract, Black Hat to convert, then return to White Hat to retain.**

Example flow:
1. **Attract** (White Hat): Epic meaning, sense of purpose
2. **Onboard** (White Hat): Early accomplishments, creativity
3. **Convert** (Black Hat): Limited offer, scarcity
4. **Retain** (White Hat): Community, ownership, continued growth

## Left Brain vs. Right Brain Drives

### The Spectrum

Octalysis also divides drives by whether they appeal to logic or emotion:

```
    LEFT BRAIN (Extrinsic)          RIGHT BRAIN (Intrinsic)
    ┌─────────────────────┐         ┌─────────────────────┐
    │ CD2: Accomplishment │         │ CD3: Creativity     │
    │ CD4: Ownership      │         │ CD5: Social         │
    │ CD6: Scarcity       │         │ CD7: Unpredictability│
    └─────────────────────┘         └─────────────────────┘
```

### Left Brain (Extrinsic Motivation)
- Logic, calculation, ownership
- "What do I get?"
- Rewards, points, possessions
- Goal-oriented behavior
- Can be satisfied/completed

### Right Brain (Intrinsic Motivation)
- Creativity, social, curiosity
- "How does this feel?"
- Experience, relationships, discovery
- Journey-oriented behavior
- Evergreen engagement

### Design Implications

**For acquisition/conversion**: Emphasize Left Brain (clear value, tangible rewards)

**For retention/engagement**: Emphasize Right Brain (experience, community, creativity)

**Ideal balance**: Start with clear extrinsic value, transition to intrinsic satisfaction

## The 4 Experience Phases

Different Core Drives matter at different stages of the user journey:

### Phase 1: Discovery
*"Why should I start?"*

**Primary Drives:**
- CD1: Epic Meaning (this matters)
- CD7: Curiosity (what is this?)
- CD5: Social Proof (others use it)

**Design Focus:**
- Compelling narrative
- Clear purpose/mission
- Social validation
- Intriguing unknowns

### Phase 2: Onboarding
*"How do I learn this?"*

**Primary Drives:**
- CD2: Accomplishment (early wins)
- CD3: Creativity (first choices)
- CD4: Ownership (first possessions)

**Design Focus:**
- Easy first wins
- Clear progress
- Meaningful early choices
- Building initial investment

### Phase 3: Scaffolding
*"Why should I keep going?"*

**Primary Drives:**
- CD3: Creativity (deeper expression)
- CD4: Ownership (growing investment)
- CD5: Social (community)
- CD6: Scarcity (exclusive access)

**Design Focus:**
- Deeper engagement mechanics
- Community integration
- Progressive unlocks
- Increased investment

### Phase 4: Endgame
*"Why should I stay forever?"*

**Primary Drives:**
- CD1: Epic Meaning (legacy)
- CD3: Creativity (mastery expression)
- CD5: Social (mentorship, leadership)

**Design Focus:**
- Mentorship opportunities
- Creative mastery
- Community leadership
- Lasting legacy

## Octalysis Score

### Calculating Your Score

Rate each Core Drive from 0-10 based on how strongly your product/experience activates it, then use this formula:

```
Score = (CD1² + CD2² + CD3² + CD4² + CD5² + CD6² + CD7² + CD8²)
```

Squaring emphasizes that strong activation of fewer drives beats weak activation of all drives.

### Score Interpretation

| Score | Interpretation |
|-------|----------------|
| 0-100 | Weak — Little motivation design |
| 100-200 | Basic — Some elements present |
| 200-400 | Moderate — Decent engagement |
| 400-600 | Strong — Well-designed experience |
| 600+ | Exceptional — World-class motivation design |

### Example Scoring

**A typical corporate training platform:**
- CD1: 2 (minimal meaning)
- CD2: 5 (some progress tracking)
- CD3: 1 (no creativity)
- CD4: 3 (basic profiles)
- CD5: 2 (limited social)
- CD6: 1 (no scarcity)
- CD7: 1 (predictable)
- CD8: 3 (completion requirements)

Score = 4 + 25 + 1 + 9 + 4 + 1 + 1 + 9 = **54** (Weak)

**A well-designed learning game:**
- CD1: 7 (meaningful narrative)
- CD2: 8 (rich progression)
- CD3: 6 (meaningful choices)
- CD4: 7 (character building)
- CD5: 6 (community features)
- CD6: 4 (limited events)
- CD7: 5 (discovery elements)
- CD8: 3 (gentle stakes)

Score = 49 + 64 + 36 + 49 + 36 + 16 + 25 + 9 = **284** (Moderate-Strong)

## Common Design Mistakes

### 1. Points, Badges, and Leaderboards (PBL) Obsession
**Mistake**: Thinking gamification = adding points and badges
**Reality**: PBL only addresses CD2 (Accomplishment). Without other drives, engagement is shallow and temporary.

### 2. All Black Hat
**Mistake**: Relying on scarcity, urgency, and loss aversion
**Reality**: Creates short-term spikes but long-term burnout and resentment.

### 3. Ignoring the Endgame
**Mistake**: Designing great onboarding but nothing for long-term users
**Reality**: Power users leave, taking their influence with them.

### 4. Meaningful Rewards for Meaningless Actions
**Mistake**: Giving badges for trivial tasks
**Reality**: Users see through hollow rewards. Achievements must represent real growth.

### 5. Forgetting the Human
**Mistake**: Optimizing for metrics instead of experiences
**Reality**: Gamification should make experiences better for users, not just for business metrics.

### 6. One-Size-Fits-All
**Mistake**: Same motivation design for all users
**Reality**: Different users respond to different drives. Achievers want CD2, Socializers want CD5, Explorers want CD7.

## Player Types & Core Drives

Different users respond to different Core Drives. Understanding player types helps target motivation design:

### Achievers
**Driven by**: CD2 (Accomplishment), CD4 (Ownership)
**Want**: Goals, progress, mastery, collection
**Design for**: Clear objectives, progress tracking, skill development

### Explorers
**Driven by**: CD7 (Curiosity), CD3 (Creativity)
**Want**: Discovery, secrets, understanding systems
**Design for**: Hidden content, depth, experimentation

### Socializers
**Driven by**: CD5 (Social), CD1 (Meaning through community)
**Want**: Connection, collaboration, belonging
**Design for**: Community features, collaboration, social recognition

### Killers/Competitors
**Driven by**: CD2 (Accomplishment vs others), CD5 (Winning)
**Want**: Competition, dominance, proving superiority
**Design for**: Leaderboards, PvP, rankings

### Philanthropists
**Driven by**: CD1 (Meaning), CD5 (Helping others)
**Want**: Purpose, helping others, making impact
**Design for**: Mentorship, contribution, meaningful impact

## Implementation Checklist

### Discovery Phase
- [ ] Clear epic meaning / mission
- [ ] Curiosity triggers (what's inside?)
- [ ] Social proof (others trust this)
- [ ] "Chosen one" feeling (destiny)

### Onboarding Phase
- [ ] Easy first win within 2 minutes
- [ ] Clear progress visualization
- [ ] Meaningful first choice
- [ ] First ownership moment
- [ ] Early feedback loop established

### Scaffolding Phase
- [ ] Multiple paths/strategies available
- [ ] Community integration
- [ ] Growing investment (time, effort, customization)
- [ ] Progressive unlocks
- [ ] Meaningful challenges (not busy work)

### Endgame Phase
- [ ] Mentorship opportunities
- [ ] Creative mastery expression
- [ ] Leadership/status roles
- [ ] Legacy/contribution features
- [ ] Continued growth paths

### Balance Check
- [ ] White Hat drives for retention
- [ ] Black Hat drives used sparingly and ethically
- [ ] Both Left Brain (rewards) and Right Brain (experience)
- [ ] Multiple Core Drives activated (not just CD2)
