# Carousel: Zone Calibration

## Slide 1 (Hook)

**Your nervous system has decided how far you're allowed to go in every area of your life.**

And you don't even know it's happening.

---

## Slide 2 (The Setup)

**Here's what I mean.**

Think about your career. Your relationships. Your creativity. Your confidence.

In each one, there's a range you operate in. A ceiling you don't go above. A floor you don't drop below.

You probably call it "just who I am."

It's not who you are. It's where your nervous system feels safe.

---

## Slide 3 (The Thesis)

**Your nervous system is a safety system.**

Its job is to keep you alive. Not to keep you fulfilled.

So when you were young and something hurt, your body learned a rule:

"Don't go there again."

That rule became a corridor. Two walls. A narrow strip of behaviour your nervous system lets you operate in.

And it runs that corridor for DECADES without you questioning it.

---

## Slide 4 (The Corridor Visual)

**Every domain of your life has one.**

[Visual: a corridor/channel with two walls]

Left wall: one failure mode (too much)
Right wall: opposite failure mode (too little)
Middle: the sweet spot (where growth happens)

Your nervous system picks a spot in this corridor and holds you there.

Not in the sweet spot. In the SAFE spot.

---

## Slide 5 (Example - Identity)

**Example: Identity**

Left wall: The Chameleon. You become whoever the room needs. You shape-shift to belong. You've been doing it so long you don't know who you actually are.

Right wall: The Outcast. You reject belonging entirely. You isolate. You tell yourself you don't need anyone.

Sweet spot: Authenticity AND belonging. Being yourself AND being welcomed for it.

Most people are pinned to one wall. Their nervous system won't let them near the other.

---

## Slide 6 (Example - Vulnerability)

**Example: Vulnerability**

Left wall: Over-sharing. You bleed on people who didn't ask. You confuse intensity with intimacy. Every interaction is a therapy session.

Right wall: The Fortress. Nothing gets in. Nobody sees the real you. You call it "being private." Your nervous system calls it "being safe."

Sweet spot: Sharing at the right depth, with the right people, at the right time.

---

## Slide 7 (The Protectors)

**So what's keeping you pinned to the wall?**

A protector. A pattern your nervous system built from an old experience.

The Ghost: hides to avoid being seen
The Controller: grips to avoid losing control
The Performer: achieves to avoid being worthless
The Perfectionist: prepares to avoid making mistakes
The People Pleaser: abandons self to avoid being abandoned

These aren't flaws. They were brilliant survival strategies.

But they've been running the show ever since.

---

## Slide 8 (The 8 Domains)

**This plays out across 8 areas of your life.**

1. Identity: Who am I really?
2. Vulnerability: Can I be honest about what I need?
3. Direction: What do I build and who do I build it for?
4. Enough: Do I have permission to move?
5. Growth: What is my real edge?
6. Execution: Can I sustain movement?
7. Passion: What do I care about enough to risk?
8. Play: Can I experience genuine play?

Each one has two walls. A sweet spot. And a protector guarding the corridor.

You might be in the sweet spot for Direction but pinned to the wall for Vulnerability.

That's normal. The work is one corridor at a time.

---

## Slide 9 (The Shift)

**The goal isn't to destroy the protector.**

It kept you safe when you needed it.

The goal is to update it. To show your nervous system that the danger it's protecting you from doesn't exist anymore.

That's not a thinking exercise. It's a body exercise.

Because the safety zone was installed in your body. Not your mind.

## Design Notes

- 10 slides (longer than typical, but the concept needs it)
- Slide 4 is the visual centrepiece: the corridor with two walls and a sweet spot diagonal
- Slides 5-6: could use split-screen or two-panel layout showing both walls
- Slide 7: show the 5 protector archetypes with their icons/cards from the app
- Slide 8: numbered list, clean, could use the level icons from LevelConfig
- Purple/gold brand colours
- No emojis in slides
- Bold for key phrases, high contrast, lots of breathing room
- The word "corridor" is the metaphor that carries the whole piece
