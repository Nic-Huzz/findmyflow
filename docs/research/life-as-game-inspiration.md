# Life as a Video Game — Inspiration for Find My Flow

**Source:** @noareserrunt Instagram Reel — "At 22, I realized life is a video game and these are the rules"

## The 10 Rules → FMF Feature Map

| # | Rule | FMF Feature | Copy / Framing Opportunity |
|---|------|------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | If you don't choose a main quest, one is chosen for you. Default path: school → 9 to 5 → marriage → mortgage → retirement. | Quest Board, Life Paths | Onboarding hook: "Choose your quest before life chooses for you" |
| 2 | Your spawn point is random. Some start with gold, others with intelligence, or luck. No rerolls. | Essence Mirror, archetypes | Reveal screen: "Here's your spawn point. Now build from it." |
| 3 | Your base stats are fixed. If you're naturally analytical, build with that. Forcing the wrong class drains energy fast. | Play Profile DNA, essence archetypes | "Forcing the wrong class drains energy" = Zone Calibration in 8 words |
| 4 | Your build is flexible. Base stats are locked but your skill tree isn't. Respecs cost time, effort, and a bit of ego. | Skill tree L0-L4, Life Map re-runs | Frame career pivots as "respecs" not failures |
| 5 | Failing isn't game over. The only real loss is quitting the run. Failures convert into practical XP. | Courage challenges, behavioral evidence | Post-failure copy: "Failures convert into practical XP, if you keep playing" |
| 6 | XP only comes from action. Reading, planning, and watching others play doesn't level you up. Reps do. Boring, unglamorous reps. | Action Score (Y axis), RP system | Surface on Journey tab. Reading/planning doesn't count. |
| 7 | Consistency beats talent. Talent is just a passive buff. Consistency carries you past most players who quit early. | Streaks, daily Tune practices | "Talent is just a passive buff" as streak motivation copy |
| 8 | It's a single-player game with co-op modes. The right partners, mentors, and friends multiply your progress. | Fantasy League, Vibe Rise sessions | Already nailed this split |
| 9 | Side quests matter more than they look. Health, relationships, curiosity, and play don't seem productive. They quietly unlock late-game perks. | Healing flow, curiosity work | Frame healing as "side quests that unlock late-game perks" |
| 10 | You decide what winning means. Money. Freedom. Mastery. Peace. There's no leaderboard. Pick your ending. | Interior Scoreboard (Clarity + Action) | Explicitly say "there's no leaderboard, you define the win" |

## Key Insight

The features are already built. What this post nails is **permission-giving language wrapped in game metaphor**. Zero jargon, zero frameworks, every line passes the 12-year-old test.

## Three Places to Apply

### 1. Onboarding Slides (pre-Essence Mirror)
Rewrite as "The Rules of Your Game." Not teaching frameworks, just giving permission.
- "You're allowed to explore all the other quests available."
- "Your spawn point was random. But your build? That's up to you."

### 2. Zarlo Wisdom Drops
These 10 rules are exactly the kind of bite-sized insight Zarlo should surface contextually:
- **Rule 6** when someone hasn't completed anything in 3 days ("XP only comes from action")
- **Rule 3** when they're forcing a quest that drains them — Stressed/Bored state ("Forcing the wrong class drains energy fast")
- **Rule 5** after a courage challenge marked as Pressure ("Failures convert into practical XP, if you keep playing")
- **Rule 7** when streak hits 7+ days ("Talent is just a passive buff. You've got something better.")
- **Rule 9** when someone completes a healing intention ("Side quests unlock late-game perks. You just found one.")

### 3. Journey Tab Empty States / Motivation Copy
Replace generic encouragement with gaming vocabulary:
- "Your skill tree is unlocked. Reps level you up, not reading."
- "No quest selected. The default path is school → 9 to 5 → retirement. Choose something better."

## Final Rule (for us)

> "Life isn't as hard as it pretends to be. You're freer than you've been told. You're allowed to choose a different path. You're allowed to dream."

This is the emotional core of Find My Flow stated without a single framework name. Consider surfacing this exact sentiment at the end of onboarding.
