## Layer 1: Dominant

**Assumption (VERIFIED):** "Security = technology. Surveillance, encryption, cybersecurity, AI monitoring, drones."

This assumption is overwhelming and nearly total. Across every subdomain of Threat (cyber, physical, military, insurance, home), the operating logic is identical: threats are external forces to be detected, deterred, and neutralised through technological systems and institutional response. The budget tells the story. The combined annual spend across these sectors exceeds $10 trillion. Virtually all of it flows toward detection hardware, response infrastructure, and risk transfer products. The idea that safety might emerge from community capacity, relational trust, or adaptive social systems receives approximately 0% of this spend.

|--------|-------------|-------|----------------------|
| Palo Alto Networks | Next-gen cybersecurity, AI-powered threat detection | $135B+ market cap, $6B+ ARR | Threats are digital, solutions are software firewalls and AI monitoring |
| Lockheed Martin | Defence contractor, weapons systems, surveillance tech | $60.3B revenue, largest defence contractor globally | National security = weapons superiority and technological dominance |
| Allied Universal | Largest private security company, AI-powered integrated security | Part of $262B private security market | Safety = guards + cameras + access control systems |
| ADT / Ring / SimpliSafe | Home security systems, smart cameras, doorbells | $57.8B US home security market; Ring holds ~16% AI citation share | Home safety = surveillance devices on your property |
| UnitedHealth / Allianz | Insurance (health, property, life, cyber) | $7-9T global insurance market | Safety = risk transfer via financial product |
| Fortinet | Network security, firewalls, endpoint protection | $60B+ valuation, $6.8B revenue (2025) | Perimeter defence: keep threats out |

**Market size:** Cybersecurity alone: $272B (2025), projected $663B by 2033. Private security: $262B (2025). Global defence: $2.75T (2026). Insurance: $7-9T (2025). Smart home security: $42-88B (2025). Combined: well over $10T annually.

**Dominant since:** The technological security paradigm has been dominant since the Cold War militarisation of the 1950s (~75 years). The digital layer accelerated from the late 1990s (firewalls, antivirus). The consumer surveillance layer (Ring, Nest, smart cameras) became mass-market from ~2016. At no point in modern history has the dominant response to "how do we stay safe?" been anything other than "buy better technology" or "fund more institutional response."

## Layer 2: Crowded

**Counter-narrative (PARTIALLY VERIFIED, BUT THIN):** "Restorative justice. Community policing. Defund-to-invest. Very early, politically charged."

Unlike Healing (where the "holistic" reaction produced a $222B market with 140K+ coaches), the Threat branch reaction is tiny, politically contested, and has largely stalled. The defund-the-police movement of 2020-2021 represented the most visible counter-narrative, but most cities reversed course within 18 months. Restorative justice remains grant-funded, not market-funded. There is no "holistic security" equivalent reaching mainstream consumers. This is the thinnest Layer 2 of any branch researched.

**Subgroups** (the reaction is fragmented and small):

1. **Restorative Justice Programs** (government/nonprofit, grant-dependent)
   - NACRJ (conference grew from 183 to 1,902 attendees, 2007-2024)
   - Impact Justice (Restorative Justice Project)
   - Equal Justice USA
   - Common Justice (alternatives to incarceration for violent felonies)
   - Community Justice for Youth Institute
   These are real but entirely grant-funded. Minnesota: $4M/yr reduced to $1M/yr in FY2026. Vermont: $1.2M allocated for first time in 2026. NYC: $6.5M one-time investment. Federal: $8M from DOJ. Total identified annual funding across all programs: under $50M nationally. Compare this to the $10T+ dominant layer.

2. **Violence Interruption / Community Safety** (public health model)
   - Cure Violence Global (evidence-based: 31-63% reduction in gun violence where implemented with fidelity)
   - READI Chicago (4:1 to 18:1 benefit-cost ratio)
   - Save Our Streets (South Bronx: 37% lower gun injuries post-implementation)
   - Advance Peace
   - Just Chicago (solidarity economy + community land trusts)
   These programs have strong evidence but remain pilot-scale. They treat violence as a public health problem, not a policing problem. Funding is fragile: federal DOJ grants were cut significantly in 2025-2026 under the new administration.

3. **Community Safety Apps** (tech-mediated neighbourhood awareness)
   - Citizen (10B+ alerts, 9M+ followers since 2017; partnered with Axon/Fusus in 2025)
   - Nextdoor (neighbourhood social network with safety features)
   - Neighbors by Ring (crowd-sourced camera footage sharing)
   - PubSafe, Noonlight, bSafe
   These digitised the neighbourhood watch concept but actually reinforced the dominant assumption: safety = surveillance + alerts. They moved the camera from the doorbell to the crowd. Citizen's 2025 partnership with Axon (police tech) confirms the convergence back toward institutional response.

4. **Personal Safety Wearables and Apps** (individual protection, primarily women)
   - Epowar (launched 2025, smartwatch stress detection + auto-emergency)
   - bSafe, Noonlight, Life360
   - Wearable panic buttons ($1.2B market in 2024, growing 12.5% CAGR)
   - Women's safety apps ($2.65B in 2025, growing 5.6% CAGR)
   These serve real needs (women's safety, elder care) but are individual-protection products. They do not build community capacity. The unit of safety remains the individual device, not the social system.

**Saturation evidence:**
- Unlike Healing's "holistic" saturation (every practitioner claims holistic), the Threat counter-movement has NOT saturated because it barely started. The defund-to-invest movement peaked in 2020 and reversed by 2022. The "Defund Cities that Defund the Police Act of 2025" was introduced in Congress, actively punishing cities that tried alternatives.
- Restorative justice conference attendance grew 10x in 17 years but remains under 2,000 people nationally. Compare this to the 109,200 ICF-certified coaches in Healing's crowded layer.
- The political charge around security alternatives is uniquely high. No other branch faces legislation designed to prevent the counter-movement from scaling.

**Class ceiling:**
- Cheapest Phase 3 option: Cure Violence community program at $0 (free to community members; fully grant-funded, not consumer-purchased). There is essentially no consumer product in this space.
- Premium Phase 3 option: Private security consulting with "resilience" framing at $5,000-50,000/engagement (enterprise, not consumer)
- Percentage priced out: Not applicable in the traditional sense. The issue is not price but availability. There are no consumer-facing Phase 3 products to buy at any price point. The counter-movement is entirely institutional (government grants, nonprofit programs). A consumer who wants "community-based safety" has no product to purchase.
- Mass-market gap: There is no product at any price point ($0-$50/mo or otherwise) that helps an individual or neighbourhood build adaptive community resilience as a security strategy. Zero. The closest products (Citizen, Nextdoor) digitised surveillance, not cohesion.

## Layer 3: The Gap

**Original prediction:** "Security as complex adaptive resilience. Systems that absorb shocks rather than prevent them. Anti-fragile communities."

**Verified?** YES. The gap is cavernous.

**Search terms used:** "adaptive resilience community platform," "anti-fragile community security," "community resilience startup app," "resilience hub security," "systems thinking community safety," "complex adaptive security community," "neighbourhood resilience platform," "social cohesion safety app"

**Players found:** 0-2 (depending on how generously you define "player")

| Player | How close to the gap | Scale | Founded |
|--------|---------------------|-------|---------|
| Resilience Hub Collaborative | Community facilities for disaster prep, not security-as-resilience | Government/nonprofit initiative, not a product | N/A (government program) |
| Cornell Resilience Hub Playbook | Launching Summer 2026; facilitated guidance for disaster resilience hubs | Academic resource, not a product | 2026 |
| Royal Society "Adaptive Security" paper | Published March 2026; proposes 6-component adaptive security framework | Academic paper, zero implementation | 2026 |

None of these are products. None are consumer-facing. None address the gap prediction: "systems that absorb shocks rather than prevent them" applied to community safety.

**Gap confidence:** HIGH (0-2 players, and arguably 0 real products)

**Barriers (why this gap exists):**

1. **Political toxicity.** Any alternative to "more police, more cameras, more tech" is politically dangerous. The defund movement's backlash made funders, entrepreneurs, and politicians allergic to anything that sounds like "less security infrastructure." The 2025 federal legislation penalising cities that reduce police budgets created an active headwind.

2. **The assumption is invisible.** "Security = technology/force" is so deeply embedded that most people cannot even conceptualise an alternative. When asked "how do you make a neighbourhood safe?", virtually everyone answers with cameras, police, lighting, locks. The idea that social cohesion, trust, mutual aid, and adaptive community capacity ARE security has no cultural vocabulary.

3. **No business model.** Healing had a clear consumer: the individual seeking therapy. Movement had a clear consumer: the commuter or fitness seeker. What is the business model for "make your neighbourhood anti-fragile"? Who pays? This is why the space is entirely grant-funded. Nobody has cracked the consumer monetisation of community resilience.

4. **Measurement problem.** Cybersecurity measures attacks blocked. Insurance measures claims paid. Community resilience measures... what? The absence of harm? The speed of recovery from disruption? Social trust indices? There is no accepted metric, so there is no market.

5. **Cultural readiness.** The concept of anti-fragility exists in Nassim Taleb's books (published 2012) and in cybersecurity strategy papers, but it has not crossed into the consumer or community space. The intellectual framework exists; the cultural readiness does not.

**Adjacent signals (what's approaching the gap from the edges):**

- **Cyber resilience** is the one domain where "absorb and adapt" (vs "prevent and block") has entered mainstream strategy. IBM, Accenture, ESET, and the World Economic Forum all publish resilience frameworks. But these apply to organisations and networks, not communities.
- **CERT volunteer programs** (Community Emergency Response Teams) train neighbourhood volunteers for disaster response. FEMA funds them. NYC has 24 divisions covering all 59 community boards. This is the closest existing infrastructure to "anti-fragile community," but it is disaster-focused, not ongoing-safety-focused.
- **Resilience Hubs** are emerging as physical community spaces for climate adaptation. The Urban Sustainability Directors Network, HUD, and multiple cities are building them. But these address environmental resilience, not social safety.
- **The Royal Society's "Adaptive Security" paper** (March 2026) proposes a shift from resilience (bounce back) to transformation (become something new that can handle what comes). Six components: entropy management, evolutionary dynamics, resource base expansion, transformation capabilities, self-governance, inter-systemic integration. This is the theoretical framework for the gap, published in a peer-reviewed journal. Zero implementation exists.

**Loop-back confidence:** MEDIUM. The data supports ancestral practices returning in modern packaging, but with a crucial distinction: almost none of the participants frame what they're doing as "security" or "threat response." The martial arts studio owner says "community" and "personal growth." The evil eye jewellery seller says "fashion" and "meaning." The sage bundle buyer says "wellness" and "energy cleansing." The ancestral protection impulse is alive and well and generating billions in revenue, but it has been culturally re-categorised. It no longer lives under "security." It lives under "wellness," "fashion," "fitness," and "spirituality."

This is the loop-back's distinctive feature in the Threat branch: the ancestral practices returned but were adopted by OTHER branches (Healing, Movement, Story) rather than reclaimed by Threat. The evil eye is "jewellery." The martial arts studio is "fitness." The sage bundle is "wellness." The protection function is present but unspoken.

## Implications for Vibe Rise / Creator Portal

This research reveals three things relevant to our positioning:

**1. The Threat frontier is the single most underserved space on the entire matrix.** Every other branch has a crowded reaction layer and multiple gap-adjacent players. Threat has neither. An experience creator operating in this space would face essentially zero competition for the Phase 3 position. This is simultaneously the biggest opportunity and the hardest sell.

**2. The "boundary" concept bridges Threat and Healing.** Our Tune tab already includes "Drains" and boundary practices. The energetic protection/boundary workshop market (Udemy, spiritual coaches) sits at the intersection of Healing and Threat. A Vibe Rise creator who teaches "boundaries as security" (emotional, energetic, relational, physical) would be operating in genuine whitespace. The language already exists in our product ("Value" focus in weekly review, "Drains" in Tune tab).

**3. The loop-back insight is the most valuable finding.** The ancestral security practices (martial arts community, protection rituals, neighbourhood mutual aid, energetic boundaries) are all alive and commercially active but have been claimed by other branches. An experience creator who explicitly reconnects these to the Threat branch ("this martial arts class is your community's security system," "this boundary ritual is how you protect yourself") would be doing genuine Phase 3 work: not inventing something new, but naming what's already happening.

**For the Creator Portal's Scale Score and branch selection:** Threat branch should be flagged as "frontier territory" with a note that Phase 3 activity is near-zero, making it the highest-opportunity branch for creators willing to work in uncomfortable, politically charged space.
