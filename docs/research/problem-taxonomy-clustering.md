## Clusters That Surprised Me

1. **Certainty as poison** emerged as its own cluster. I expected "certainty" tags to scatter across politics and science, but they held together tightly. Bronowski, Szymborska, McGilchrist, Berlin, Bohm, Munger: these people all point at the same underlying brokenness from completely different disciplines.

2. **Children not being seen** turned out to be much larger than expected (42 tags). The dataset contains an unusually rich set of people who dedicated their lives specifically to how adults fail children, spanning pedagogy, psychology, wartime protection, and attachment theory.

3. **Work that breaks people** attracted founder tags naturally. Cucinelli, Schultz, Sinegal, Graeber, Terkel all point at the same thing from different angles: work that strips meaning and dignity. This is the cluster most likely to resonate with FindMyFlow's burnt-out professional audience.

## Open Questions for Human Review

1. **Should "Voices being silenced" split?** At 52 tags, it is the second-largest cluster. It contains both political silencing (Havel, Sosa, Politkovskaya) and personal/trauma silencing (Angelou, Burke). These feel different on the user's end. A split into "Voices silenced by power" and "Voices lost to trauma" might be worth testing.

2. **The women problem.** Women being dismissed, exploited, stolen from, and pushed aside runs through at least 8 clusters. Should there be a separate "Women pushed aside" cluster, or does it stay distributed? Pulling it out would gut several other clusters. But women experiencing this cross-cutting pattern might want to see it named.

3. **Design and craft.** Tags about gardens (Oudolf, Jekyll), furniture (Eames, Rams), cities (Jacobs, Gehl, Lynch), and typography (Vignelli, Scher) are currently spread across "Creative work dismissed," "Systems that don't see you," and "Knowledge locked away." A dedicated "Things made badly" cluster would be interesting but might be too niche for a general audience.

4. **Grief.** Grief tags (Patti Smith, Rumi, Elaine Pagels, Kurosawa) are parked in "Meaning that got lost." If the dataset grows, grief may deserve its own home.

5. **Founder skew.** Founder tags lean heavily toward business/work themes, creating a natural pull toward "Work that breaks people" and "Knowledge locked away." The non-founder tags are more varied. This means the taxonomy may feel more "complete" for non-founders than founders unless founder-specific clusters are deliberately balanced.

6. **"Power crushing people" is the biggest cluster (56 tags).** It holds together because the underlying brokenness is clear: power used to dominate. But it spans slavery, authoritarianism, voter suppression, colonialism, and labour exploitation. Consider whether a user would recognise all of these as "the same problem I care about" or whether it should split along lines like "People being owned" vs "People being governed into silence."
