# P3 Business Types Framework + Hormozi Integration

## Overview

This document outlines how Seth Godin's P3 Business Types framework integrates with Alex Hormozi's $100M Offers methodology to create a more powerful offer-building system.

**Goal**: Help users identify their natural business type based on the problems they solve, then tailor their money model stack accordingly.

**Status**: Framework designed, implementation pending.

---

## Part 1: Seth Godin's P3 Business Types

### The Four Types

| Type | Core Definition | Key Question | Example |
|------|----------------|--------------|---------|
| **Jobber** | Take something from a factory, do something special for a small group of people | "What can I make special for a specific audience?" | Premium courses, curated products, boutique services |
| **Coordinator** | Connect people who want to be connected | "Who wants to be connected but isn't?" | Conferences, communities, directories, masterminds |
| **Labor Organizer** | Know the labor AND know who needs it, create value by connecting them | "What labor can I connect to those who need it?" | Agencies, staffing, freelancer marketplaces, talent placement |
| **Asset Owner** | Own an asset/device that makes life easier for people who use it | "What asset can I own that makes life easier?" | SaaS tools, equipment rental, platforms, software |

### Key Insight from Seth Godin

> "It doesn't have to be personal. And it doesn't have to be something you'd buy from you. It can simply be something that's useful, irresistible and profitable."

### Detailed Breakdown

#### 1. JOBBER
**What they do**: Take something that exists and make it special for a specific audience.

**The trap**: "I'll be a little cheaper than everyone else" - this fails because anyone can do that.

**The win**: Be SPECIFIC. Content with small and focused instead of large and generic.

**Examples**:
- Moo.com + Seth Godin partnership (premium business cards for specific audience)
- Reading glasses: Chinese factories + Shopify + premium positioning
- Turkey Hill Farm maple syrup at farmers market (experience premium)
- Curated subscription boxes for specific niches

**Value creation**: The curation, the positioning, the specificity, the experience.

#### 2. COORDINATOR (Impresario)
**What they do**: Get the right people in the room. Being in the room becomes valuable.

**Key insight**: If it was only one person or one group, it wouldn't work. The value is in the NETWORK.

**Where competition creates value**: Job fairs, directories, trade shows, conferences.

**Examples**:
- TED conferences
- Industry directories
- Job fairs
- Masterminds
- Seth's law firm directory (charged firms to feature, gave to law graduates)

**Value creation**: Access, connections, being in proximity to the right people.

#### 3. LABOR ORGANIZER
**What they do**: If you know the labor and the labor trusts you, AND you know who needs the labor and they trust you - you create value for both.

**The hard part**: Finding someone who needs the labor bad enough but isn't willing to go through all the steps to coordinate it themselves.

**Examples**:
- Staffing agencies
- Freelancer marketplaces (Upwork, Fiverr)
- Recruiting firms
- Talent agencies
- Virtual assistant placement services

**Value creation**: Trust on both sides, vetting, matching, speed.

#### 4. ASSET OWNER
**What they do**: Own something that makes life easier for people who use it. They either rent it or you use the asset for them.

**Examples**:
- Snow plow service (instead of people shoveling)
- SaaS tools
- Equipment rental
- Platforms
- Automation tools

**Value creation**: Convenience, time savings, capability they couldn't afford to own.

## Part 2: Mapping to Hormozi's Money Models

### The Hormozi Stack

| Layer | Purpose | Conversion |
|-------|---------|------------|
| **Lead Magnet** | Capture attention, build trust | Stranger → Lead |
| **Core Offer** | Main transformation | Lead → Customer |
| **Upsell** | More/faster/better for buyers | Customer → Premium |
| **Downsell** | Alternative for non-buyers | "No" → "Maybe this?" |
| **Continuity** | Recurring relationship | One-time → Recurring |

### How Each Business Type Stacks Differently

#### JOBBER Stack
*"Take something, make it special for a specific audience"*

| Layer | Natural Expression | Example |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| Lead Magnet | Sample, assessment, buyer's guide | "Free skincare analysis" |
| Core Offer | Premium curated product/service | Custom skincare routine ($297) |
| Upsell | Done-for-you, VIP tier, bundle | Quarterly auto-ship + consults ($147/mo) |
| Downsell | DIY version, templates, basic | "Just the products" ($97) |
| Continuity | Replenishment, membership | Monthly product box |

**Strongest lever**: Proof Stack (why YOUR curation is special)
**Continuity challenge**: Must CREATE membership/replenishment - not natural

#### COORDINATOR Stack
*"Connect people who want to be connected"*

| Layer | Natural Expression | Example |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| Lead Magnet | Free community tier, guest pass | "Join our free Slack" |
| Core Offer | Paid access to network/event | Annual conference ticket ($997) |
| Upsell | VIP access, mastermind, 1:1 intros | VIP dinner + speaker access ($2,997) |
| Downsell | Virtual pass, recordings only | Livestream only ($297) |
| Continuity | Membership dues | Monthly mastermind ($297/mo) |

**Strongest lever**: Network effects (who else is in the room?)
**Continuity advantage**: Membership is NATURAL

#### LABOR ORGANIZER Stack
*"Connect labor with those who need it"*

| Layer | Natural Expression | Example |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| Lead Magnet | Free job post, talent preview | "Post 1 job free" |
| Core Offer | Placement/matching service | Hire a vetted VA ($1,500 placement) |
| Upsell | Exclusive talent, rush, managed | "We manage them for you" ($997/mo) |
| Downsell | Self-service, job board access | DIY talent database ($97/mo) |
| Continuity | Retainer, talent pipeline | Ongoing recruitment retainer |

**Strongest lever**: Speed + Trust (pre-vetted, guaranteed)
**Continuity model**: Retainers or ongoing placement fees

#### ASSET OWNER Stack
*"Own something that makes life easier"*

| Layer | Natural Expression | Example |
|-------|-------------------|---------|
| Lead Magnet | Free trial, freemium tier | "14-day free trial" |
| Core Offer | Access to the tool/asset | Pro plan ($49/mo) |
| Upsell | Premium features, white-glove setup | Enterprise + onboarding ($299/mo) |
| Downsell | Limited version, annual discount | Basic plan ($19/mo) or annual ($399/yr) |
| Continuity | SaaS subscription (built-in!) | Monthly/annual plans |

**Strongest lever**: Ease Factor (done-for-you automation)
**Continuity advantage**: CORE OFFER IS CONTINUITY

### Continuity Fit by Type

| Type | Continuity Fit | Why |
|------|---------------|-----|
| Asset Owner | ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ Native | Tool access IS the continuity |
| Coordinator | ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Strong | Community membership is natural |
| Labor Organizer | ⭐⭐⭐ Medium | Retainers work, but project-based is common |
| Jobber | ⭐⭐ Harder | Must create replenishment or membership layer |

**This explains why:**
- SaaS founders (Asset Owners) have high valuations (recurring built-in)
- Course creators (Jobbers) struggle with churn (have to manufacture continuity)
- Community builders (Coordinators) can scale with membership
- Agencies (Labor Organizers) often stay project-based unless they add retainers

## Part 3: The Problem → Business Type Bridge

### Current Problem: Q7 Doesn't Map to Business Types

Current Offer Builder V1 Q7 captures "Why They Haven't Solved It":
- Internal Beliefs (3+ reasons)
- External Blockers (3+ reasons)
- Motivation Gaps (3+ reasons)

**Issue**: These capture WHY they're stuck, not WHAT they fundamentally need.

### Proposed: Problem Gap Categories

Reframe Q7 to capture the **nature of the gap**:

| Gap Category | Maps to Type | What It Captures |
|--------------|--------------|------------------|
| **Quality Gap** | Jobber | "Existing options are generic/low-quality" |
| **Access Gap** | Coordinator | "Missing connections, isolated, need network" |
| **Talent Gap** | Labor Organizer | "Can't find good help, need skilled people" |
| **Capacity Gap** | Asset Owner | "Takes too long, need to automate" |

### New Q7 Structure

#### Quality Gaps (Jobber Signals)
*"What's wrong with existing solutions they've tried?"*

Prompt: "List reasons why existing solutions haven't worked for them"

Signal phrases to detect:
- "Too generic"
- "Not specialized enough"
- "Low quality"
- "One-size-fits-all doesn't work"
- "Need something tailored"
- "Existing options are mass-market"

#### Access Gaps (Coordinator Signals)
*"What connections or opportunities are they missing?"*

Prompt: "What people, networks, or opportunities do they lack access to?"

Signal phrases to detect:
- "Don't know the right people"
- "Isolated"
- "Not in the right rooms"
- "Missing connections"
- "Need introductions"
- "Want to meet others like them"

#### Talent Gaps (Labor Organizer Signals)
*"What help can't they find?"*

Prompt: "What skilled help do they need but can't find or afford?"

Signal phrases to detect:
- "Can't find good help"
- "Don't know where to find X"
- "Hiring is hard"
- "Need skilled people"
- "Tried freelancers but quality varies"
- "Don't have time to vet people"

#### Capacity Gaps (Asset Owner Signals)
*"What's taking too much time or effort?"*

Prompt: "What tasks are eating their time that could be automated or simplified?"

Signal phrases to detect:
- "Takes too long"
- "Doing manually"
- "Need to automate"
- "Repetitive tasks"
- "Don't have the tools"
- "Wish there was software for this"

### How Distribution Reveals Business Type

```
Example User's Answers:
├── Quality Gaps:   ████████░░ 4 answers  → JOBBER signal (strong)
├── Access Gaps:    ██░░░░░░░░ 1 answer   → Coordinator (weak)
├── Talent Gaps:    █░░░░░░░░░ 0 answers  → Labor Org (none)
└── Capacity Gaps:  ███░░░░░░░ 2 answers  → Asset Owner (moderate)

AI Recommendation: "You're a JOBBER with Asset Owner tendencies.
Your core offer should be premium/specialized, and consider
building tools or templates as part of your value stack."
```

## Appendix: Original Seth Godin P3 Source

From Seth Godin's P3 Workshop (Bootstrapping):

### Jobbers
> "Take something a factory makes, and offer it to a select audience in a way that's more delightful, more efficient or more effective than they can do without you. This sounds simple, and it's tempting to build a very thin wall of difference. It seems easy to buy a product at wholesale, build a website to sell it, mark the price up and create some sort of 'passive' income. But that almost never happens because anyone can do that, so they will, and the price war will begin. Instead, the objective here is that you can do this effectively if you are SPECIFIC, if you are content with small and focused instead of large and generic."

### Coordinators
> "Generate value by connecting people together. Common examples are a conference like TED, a directory, and a job fair. If it was only one person or one group, it wouldn't work."

### Labor Organizers
> "If you know the labor and the labor trusts you, you are providing a huge service to them. And, if you know the person who needs the labor and they trust you, you are providing a huge service to them. The hard part is finding someone who needs the labor bad enough but isn't willing to go through all the steps to coordinate the labor."

### Asset Owners
> "You own an asset, a device, or a thing that makes life easier for the people who use it. Either they use it by renting it or because you use the asset for them."
