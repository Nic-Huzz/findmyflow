# Find My Flow — Framework Overview

*Original IP: Huzz Hurrell / FindMyFlow*

---

## The Insight

Everyone sees the problem they want to solve at a level of specificity that matches their self-knowledge.

Not their intelligence. Not their ambition. Their self-knowledge.

Someone with low self-knowledge sees broad: "I want to help people live authentically." Someone with high self-knowledge sees specific: "I help therapists translate clinical jargon into language their clients can actually use."

These aren't different problems. They're the same problem seen through different lenses. The lens is self-knowledge.

## The Two Directions

The journey moves in BOTH directions depending on where you are:

**Chunking DOWN (starting out):** You start broad because you can't see the specific version yet. Not because you chose wrong, but because your self-knowledge hasn't focused enough to see it. The narrowing IS the self-discovery.

**Chunking UP (growing):** You start specific because you found your thing. As you build evidence and capability, your scope broadens organically. You can serve bigger problems because you solved the specific one first. The broadening IS the growth.

## The River System

A 2x2 with four stages, following water from its first channel to its full flow.

**X axis:** Self-Knowledge (low to high)
**Y axis:** Problem Scope (specific to broad)

### The Stream (specific + low self-knowledge)
You're moving fast in a narrow channel, but it's not your water. Someone else dug this channel and you're flowing through it because the banks were already there. You're skilled at navigating it. You could do it with your eyes closed. That's the problem. You ARE doing it with your eyes closed. This is where most burnt-out professionals are before the breakdown.

### The Lake (broad + low self-knowledge)
The stream found a basin and everything pooled. You're full. Self-knowledge pouring in from every direction. Courses, healing, journals, assessments. Deep but still. Everything flows IN, nothing flows OUT. You can see the sky reflected in you but you have no outlet. This feels productive. It's actually formlessness.

### The Waterfall (specific + high self-knowledge)
The lake found its edge. All that accumulated depth drops into something narrow, powerful, specific. You can't have a waterfall without the lake above it. The self-knowledge HAD to accumulate first. But the waterfall is the moment it all focuses. It's small. It's narrow. But it's YOURS. Not borrowed, not assigned, not performed.

### The River (broad + high self-knowledge)
The waterfall feeds a river. It widens as tributaries join. But it has direction because it came from a specific waterfall which came from a full lake. The breadth is earned. You never lost the source. You just grew from it.

## The Journey

Stream → Lake → Waterfall → River

Each transition maps to a real moment:
- **Stream → Lake** = The Crack. You leave someone else's channel. Everything pools.
- **Lake → Waterfall** = The hardest move. All that depth finding its specific edge. This is where the real work happens.
- **Waterfall → River** = Earned expansion. Organic, not forced.

"**Find My Flow**" = find your waterfall. The specific, powerful outlet where your depth becomes direction. Then let it flow.

## The Pre/Trans Trap

The most important concept in the framework.

The Lake and The River look identical from the outside. Both are wide. Both hold a lot. Both sound visionary. The difference is invisible to anyone who hasn't done the journey.

The Lake says "everyone deserves transformation" because it sounds right. The River says it because they've delivered transformation to specific people and watched it work.

**The diagnostic question:** "Can you explain your problem at three levels of specificity, niche, medium, and universal, with equal conviction and equal evidence?" If you can, you're in The River. If the niche level feels forced, you're in The Lake performing breadth.

## What Makes This Different

Every existing framework treats "start narrow" as a business strategy:
- Hormozi says niche down to charge more
- Moore says start with one bowling pin market
- Godin says find the smallest viable audience

Find My Flow says: **you start broad because you don't know yourself yet, not because you chose wrong.** The narrowing isn't a strategy. It's a developmental journey. And the subsequent broadening isn't a strategic choice. It's an earned capacity that only comes from having done the specific work first.

Nobody else maps the return journey, from specific back to broad, as a developmental achievement rather than a strategic choice.

## Application

The framework helps someone answer three questions:
1. **Where am I?** Which stage of the river system am I in right now?
2. **What's the next move?** Each stage has a specific prescription.
3. **Am I fooling myself?** The Pre/Trans Trap catches people who think they're in The River when they're actually in The Lake.
