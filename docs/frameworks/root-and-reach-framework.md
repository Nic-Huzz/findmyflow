# Find My Flow
*Problem Scope as a Personal Development Journey — Original IP: Huzz Hurrell / FindMyFlow. April 2026.*

---

## The Insight

Everyone sees the problem they want to solve at a level of specificity that matches their self-knowledge. Not their intelligence. Not their ambition. Their self-knowledge.

- **Niche/focused**: "Healing wisdom is locked inside clinical jargon nobody outside therapy rooms can access." Solvable. Specific enough to start a business around.
- **Medium scope**: "People are locked out of transformation tools because of cost and complexity." Serves more people. Grows the business.
- **Broad/universal**: "Systems teach people to disconnect from themselves." Big vision. Too vague to start with.

These are NOT three different problems. They're the same problem seen through three different lenses. The lens is self-knowledge.

**The core principle:** The journey moves in BOTH directions depending on where you are.

**Starting out (unclear on yourself):** You start broad ("I care about people being stuck") and need to chunk DOWN to find your specific starting point. This is a clarity challenge. Self-knowledge is low, so you can't see the specific problem yet.

**Growing (clear on yourself):** You start specific ("I help therapists translate clinical jargon into accessible language") and chunk UP to solve bigger problems and serve more people. This is a capability challenge. You know exactly what you do. Now you scale the scope.

The narrowing IS the self-discovery. The broadening IS the growth. Neither is a strategic business choice. Both are developmental moves.

## The River System

The framework uses a river metaphor. The journey follows water from its first channel to its full flow: **Stream → Lake → Waterfall → River.**

**X axis:** Self-Knowledge (low to high) — how well you understand which specific problem is yours
**Y axis:** Problem Scope (specific to broad) — how wide the problem you're trying to solve

```
     Broad    THE LAKE ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─  THE RIVER
               ↑                                     ↑
               │ The Crack                            │ Earned

               │  stream)                             │
               │                                      │
     Specific  THE STREAM ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ ─ → THE WATERFALL
                          self-knowledge work
              Low SK                              High SK
```

## The Four Stages

### The Stream (specific + low self-knowledge)

**Where most burnt-out professionals START, before the Crack.**

You're moving fast in a narrow channel, but it's not your water. Someone else dug this channel and you're flowing through it because the banks were already there. You're skilled at navigating it. You could do it with your eyes closed. That's the problem. You ARE doing it with your eyes closed.

You didn't choose this channel. The terrain shaped you. You're a management consultant who optimises supply chains. A lawyer who structures M&A deals. A developer who builds SaaS dashboards. You're skilled. You're credible. And something about it has stopped making sense.

This maps to the problem category "Work that hollows you out." That's not a coincidence. This IS the stage where that problem lives.

**The feeling:** Competent but numb. "I'm good at this but I don't know why I'm doing it."

**The danger:** Staying because the banks are comfortable. The golden handcuffs. Mistaking market validation for personal alignment.

**Protective archetypes here:** Performer (keeps producing because output = worth), Controller (holds tight because letting go means facing the void).

---

### The Lake (broad + low self-knowledge)

**Where most people land AFTER the Crack.**

The stream found a basin and everything pooled. You're full. Self-knowledge pouring in from every direction. Courses, healing, journals, assessments. Deep but still. Everything flows IN, nothing flows OUT. You can see the sky reflected in you but you have no outlet.

"I want to help people." "I care about transformation." "I believe everyone deserves to live authentically." These statements are true. They're also useless as a starting point.

This IS Head Full of Dreams in water form. Self-knowledge is flooding in (you're doing every assessment, every journal, every healing modality) but it hasn't found its edge yet. You can see what you care about, just not at a resolution that's actionable.

**The feeling:** Overwhelmed by possibility. "I care about everything. How do I pick?"

**The danger:** Permanent stillness. The Lake feels productive because you're "filling up." You're attending workshops, consuming content about yourself, resonating with big ideas. But accumulation without an outlet is just avoidance wearing curiosity's clothes.

**Protective archetypes here:** Perfectionist (waits until the "right" outlet reveals itself, which never quite arrives), Ghost (explores from the sidelines without committing to any specific direction).

---

### The Waterfall (specific + high self-knowledge)

**The destination of the self-knowledge work. Where the lake finds its edge.**

All that accumulated depth drops into something narrow, powerful, specific. You can't have a waterfall without the lake above it. The self-knowledge HAD to accumulate first. But the waterfall is the moment it all focuses. Concentrated. High-impact. Unmistakably going somewhere.

You found YOUR specific problem. Not the market's. Not your parents'. Not the one that pays the most. The one that's actually yours.

From the outside, The Waterfall looks similar to The Stream. Both are narrow. Both involve focused energy in a specific channel. The difference is SOURCE. The Stream flows through someone else's channel. The Waterfall drops from YOUR accumulated depth. The Stream runs on terrain. The Waterfall runs on self-knowledge.

"I help therapists translate clinical jargon into accessible language" is a Waterfall statement if it came from your own wound, your own skills, your own people. It's a Stream statement if someone hired you to do it.

**The feeling:** Quiet certainty. "This is mine. I know why I'm here."

**The danger:** Premature widening. The pressure to go broader comes fast, from the market, from your ego, from well-meaning advisors who say "you're thinking too small." The Waterfall is where you build the evidence base that earns you The River later. Leaving too early means you widen without depth behind you.

**Protective archetypes here:** Performer (gets impatient with niche scale, wants bigger numbers), Controller (tries to engineer expansion before it's organic).

---

### The River (broad + high self-knowledge)

**Earned breadth. Your waterfall became something bigger.**

The waterfall feeds a river. It widens as tributaries join. But it has direction because it came from a specific waterfall which came from a full lake. The breadth is earned. You never lost the source. You just grew from it.

Your therapist-language work became a methodology for any expert locked in jargon. Then a movement about accessible knowledge. Then a philosophy about who owns wisdom. Each widening was grounded because it grew from a waterfall, not imagined in a lake.

The River looks identical to The Lake from the outside. Both are wide. Both hold a lot. The difference is movement. The Lake is still. The River flows. The Lake is broad because it can't find an outlet. The River is broad because it started from a specific source and grew.

This is Ken Wilber's Pre/Trans distinction applied to entrepreneurial scope. Pre-rational breadth (The Lake) LOOKS LIKE trans-rational breadth (The River), but they're fundamentally different. The Lake is broad because it can't discriminate. The River is broad because it HAS discriminated and then re-integrated.

**The feeling:** Expansive purpose. "I see the whole landscape AND know exactly where I stand in it."

**The danger:** Losing the source. Going so broad you forget the waterfall that started it. The best River-stage builders keep one foot in the specific work.

## The Journey

The journey follows water's natural path: **Stream → Lake → Waterfall → River.**

### Move 1: Stream to Lake (The Crack)

**You leave someone else's channel. Everything pools.**

You were flowing in a narrow channel you didn't carve. Then the constructed life broke. You stop flowing in someone else's direction. Everything pools. Self-knowledge floods in. You go broad because you've lost the thing that was specific.

This move feels like dissolution. "I don't want to be a management consultant anymore. I want to HELP PEOPLE." It feels like growth. It's actually freefall with a purpose.

The Crack doesn't always move you to The Lake. Some people leave one Stream and enter another (sideways, still specific, still not theirs). The upward move, the broadening, requires willingness to sit in not-knowing.

### Move 2: Lake to Waterfall (The Hardest Move)

**All that depth finds its specific edge.**

Each Flow Finder session, each problem identification, each "that's me" moment brings the lake closer to its edge. You're trading breadth for focus. Universal care for specific conviction.

This phase FEELS like loss. "Am I really going to be the person who just helps therapists write accessible content? I wanted to change the world!" The narrowing triggers every protective archetype. The Performer says "think bigger." The Controller says "this is too risky, too niche." The Perfectionist says "what if it's the wrong problem?" The Ghost says "maybe I should keep filling up."

The narrowing is the hardest move because it requires killing possibilities. Every specific choice is a thousand broad dreams you're setting down. The framework's job is to reframe this: you're not losing breadth. You're gaining power. A waterfall is more powerful than a lake BECAUSE it's narrow.

**The tools that drive this move:** Flow Finder (Skills, Problems, Personas), Mind Space (primary combination), Essence Mirror (who you are underneath the wound), Tension Assessment (what's actually blocking you), and the experiences themselves (see The Experience Engine below).

### Move 3: Waterfall to River (Earned Expansion)

**Your specificity becomes a platform.**

With your Waterfall established, scope broadens organically. Not because you decided to scale, but because the specific work reveals the universal pattern underneath it.

You helped therapists translate jargon. You noticed it wasn't just therapists, it was every expert who cared more about being right than being understood. The problem expanded because you saw it clearly at the specific level first.

This move is capability-driven, not clarity-driven. You're not discovering anything new about yourself. You're building capacity to serve the bigger version of what you already know is yours.

**The tools that drive this move:** Business stages (validation, offer creation, product ladder), CRM (scaling relationships), the Diagonal itself (action proportional to self-knowledge, now both axes moving).

## The Scope Diagnostic

### Three Questions

Three free-text questions designed so the structure itself reveals the user's stage. If they CAN be specific, the question draws it out. If they can't, they naturally stay general. The gap IS the diagnostic.

**Q1: What problem do you solve?**
> If you can, name the specific type of person who has it and what it looks like in their daily life.

| Stage | What the answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Stream | Industry-specific, sounds like a LinkedIn headline. "I help mid-size companies reduce churn." |
| Lake | Broad, aspirational, could be anyone's. "I want to help people live more authentically." |
| Waterfall | Specific AND personal, connected to lived experience. "I help coaches who freeze the moment someone asks them to charge what they're worth." |
| River | Naturally gives you multiple levels. "I make expert knowledge accessible, starting with therapists who can't translate clinical jargon." |

**Q2: What happened that made this personal for you?**
> If there was a specific moment, describe it.

| Stage | What the answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Stream | Market logic. "Because I have 10 years of experience in this space." |
| Lake | Emotional but vague. "I've just always cared about this." |
| Waterfall | Wound-connected. "I was the coach who froze. I undercharged for two years because I didn't believe my work was worth it." |
| River | Evidence-based. "I solved it at the specific level and now I see the pattern everywhere." |

**Q3: What's happening in their life right now that would make them come to you, specifically? And how do you know?**

| Stage | What the answer sounds like |
|---|---|
| Stream | Client-defined. "Companies with 50-200 employees experiencing growth pain." |
| Lake | Universal. "Anyone who feels stuck or disconnected." |
| Waterfall | Felt and specific. "Women who just left corporate and are terrified their healing work isn't 'real' enough to charge for. I know because I was them." |
| River | Pattern-level. "Every expert who cares more about being right than being understood. I've now worked with therapists, architects, and academics who all have the same block." |

### Classification Logic

The three questions map to three dimensions:

| Question | Dimension | Stream signal | Lake signal | Waterfall signal |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1: What problem | Scope | Market-specific | Universal | Felt + specific |
| Q2: Why yours | Source | Credentials | Emotion without origin | Wound-connected |
| Q3: Who + how you know | Audience + evidence | Client-defined | Abstract | Felt + "I was them" |

All three pointing the same direction = high confidence classification. Mixed signals = classifier weights Q2 (source) highest, because ownership source is the truest discriminator between stages.

### Self-Assessment Version

At any point, ask yourself: **"Can I explain my problem at three levels of specificity, niche, medium, and universal, with equal conviction and equal evidence?"**

| Answer | Location | Prescription |
|--------|----------|-------------|
| I can only do broad | The Lake | Stop filling up. Start committing. Pick one problem and find the edge. |
| I can only do specific, but it doesn't feel like mine | The Stream | Your channel is a clue, not a prison. What about it lights you up? What doesn't? |
| I can only do specific, and it IS mine | The Waterfall | Stay here longer than you want to. Build the evidence base. |
| I can do all three with equal clarity | The River | You've earned breadth. Build the movement. |
| I can do broad AND specific but the connection feels forced | The Lake pretending to be The River | Your broad vision hasn't grown from your specific work. It was pasted on top. Go back to The Waterfall. |

## The Experience Engine (Fog-to-Root Mechanism)

The diagnostic tells users where they are. The **experiences they create** are what moves them.

Each experience a user creates in the /create tab is a hypothesis about their Waterfall. They pick a topic. They pick an audience. They run it. They reflect. The 3% note captures what they learned. Over multiple cycles, their scope narrows because reality narrows it for them.

### How it works

**Experience 1** (Lake state): "Breathwork Workshop" for "anyone interested in wellness"
**Post-event reflection:** "The corporate women were the ones who really got it. The yoga crowd already knew this stuff."
**3% note:** "Next time, speak directly to the corporate crowd."

**Experience 2** (narrowing): "Breathwork for Burnout" for "professionals experiencing burnout"
**Post-event reflection:** "The ones who'd just quit their jobs were the most engaged. The ones still in jobs were checked out."
**3% note:** "Focus on people in the transition, not still stuck."

**Experience 3** (Waterfall emerging): "Somatic Reset for Women Leaving Corporate" for "women who just quit and don't know what's next"
**Post-event reflection:** "This is my room. I know exactly what they need because I was them."

### What the app tracks

The Scope Map re-runs periodically (every 3 experiences, or on demand) and compares:

1. **Answer trajectory** — Are the 3 diagnostic answers getting more specific over time? AI compares previous vs. current responses.
2. **Experience specificity** — Are the experience names and audiences trending narrower? The metadata already exists in the experiences table.
3. **Reflection patterns** — Do the 3% notes point toward a consistent audience or problem?

**Output:** "Your first experience was for 'anyone interested in breathwork.' Your last two were for 'women leaving corporate.' You're moving from The Lake to The Waterfall."

This turns the /create tab from a checklist tool into a **Waterfall-finding engine.** The experiences are the experiments. The reflections are the data. The Scope Map tracks the trajectory.

## The Pre/Trans Trap

The most important concept in this framework. Borrowed from Wilber, applied to problem-scope.

**The Lake and The River look identical from the outside.** Both are wide. Both hold a lot. Both sound visionary. The difference is invisible to anyone who hasn't done the journey.

This matters because:

1. **The Lake mistakes itself for The River.** "I care about human disconnection" feels like thought leadership. It's actually unfocused empathy. The person hasn't found their waterfall, so they can't tell the difference between genuine breadth and developmental avoidance.

2. **The market can't tell the difference.** Social media rewards broad, inspirational statements. The Lake gets engagement. The River gets engagement. Followers can't distinguish "broad because they haven't found their thing yet" from "broad because they mastered their thing and expanded."

3. **Only The Waterfall reveals the truth.** If someone can articulate their problem at niche, medium, and universal levels with equal specificity and equal evidence, they're in The River. If the niche level feels forced or borrowed, they're in The Lake performing breadth.

**The diagnostic question for the Pre/Trans Trap:** "What's happening in their life right now that would make them come to you, specifically? And how do you know?"

River answers with specifics from lived experience. Lake answers with principles, aspirations, hypotheticals.

## Relationship to Direction Sweet Spot

The Scope Map and the Direction Sweet Spot (Level 3, Zone Calibration Framework) measure overlapping territory but serve different purposes.

| | Direction Sweet Spot | Scope Map |
|---|---|---|
| **Y axis** | Specificity (how concrete your direction is) | Problem Scope (how broad) |
| **X axis** | Authenticity (essence vs. constructed self) | Self-Knowledge (how well you know yourself) |
| **Shape** | Diagonal (3 zones) | 2x2 (4 stages) |
| **Purpose** | Repair graph. Fixes the wound of constructed direction. | Diagnostic tool. Maps where you are in problem-scope clarity. |
| **When** | Level 3, within the journey progression | /create entry point, before building |
| **Question** | "Is your essence in your work?" | "How clearly can you see your specific problem?" |

**The relationship is causal:** Doing the Direction Sweet Spot work is what moves someone from Lake to Waterfall on the Scope Map. When you repair constructed direction at Level 3 (bring your essence into your service), your problem-scope gets specific AND authentic.

Direction Sweet Spot is the engine. Scope Map is the dashboard.

```
Scope Map position    →    What moves them             →    New position

The Stream            →    The Crack                   →    The Lake
The Lake              →    Level 3: Direction SS work   →    The Waterfall
                           + creating experiences
The Waterfall         →    Levels 5-8: Build phases     →    The River
```

## In-App Implementation

### First-time /create visitor (no experiences)

The empty state of ExperienceCatalog becomes the Scope Map diagnostic:

1. **Hook** — "Before you create anything, let's figure out what's yours to build." The Scope Map graph, empty.
2. **Q1** — What problem do you solve?
3. **Q2** — What happened that made this personal?
4. **Q3** — What's happening in their life that would make them come to you? How do you know?
5. **AI Classification** — Haiku reads responses + existing data (problem category spread, tension scores). Classifies into stage.
6. **Reveal** — Dot appears on the Scope Map. Stage name, description, reframe.
7. **Confirmation** — "Does this sound like where you are?" with brief descriptions of all 4 stages. Manual override if wrong.
8. **Prescription** — Stage-specific next step:

| Stage | Prescription |
|---|---|
| **The Stream** | Show existing skills/problems from /get-started with ownership reframe: "Which of these feel like play, not just competence?" If unhappy with answers, route to redo Flow Finder. |
| **The Lake** | "You care about everything. Pick ONE problem for your first experience." Surface top 2-3 problem categories, ask to commit. One confirmation screen with their Waterfall statement, then into ExperienceCreate. |
| **The Waterfall** | "You found your thing. Let's build." Straight to ExperienceCreate with root problem pre-populated. |
| **The River** | Stubbed for v1. Normal "+ New Experience" flow. |

### Returning visitor (has experiences)

Summary card at top of ExperienceCatalog showing:
- Current stage
- Their Waterfall statement (or "still finding it")
- Experience trajectory: "Your last 3 experiences were for [audience]. You're [moving toward the Waterfall / building Waterfall evidence / expanding into The River]."
- Tappable to retake diagnostic.

### Database

New `scope_map_results` table:

```sql
create table scope_map_results (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  user_id uuid references auth.users(id) not null,
  stage text not null check (stage in ('stream', 'lake', 'waterfall', 'river')),
  response_problem text,
  response_source text,
  response_audience text,
  ai_reasoning text,
  problem_spread int,
  created_at timestamptz default now()
);
```

Multiple rows per user to track movement over time.

## What's Original

Every existing framework treats "start narrow" as a business strategy:
- Hormozi says niche down to charge more.
- Moore says start with one bowling pin market.
- Godin says find the smallest viable audience.

Find My Flow says: **you start broad because you don't know yourself yet, not because you chose wrong.** The narrowing isn't a strategy. It's a developmental journey. And the subsequent broadening isn't a growth hack. It's an earned capacity that only comes from having done the specific work first.

Nobody else maps the return journey, from specific back to broad, as a developmental achievement rather than a strategic choice. The broad-before-specific person (The Lake) is in a qualitatively different state than the broad-after-specific person (The River), even though they look identical from the outside.

## External Framework Comparison

| Framework | What it captures | What it misses |
|-----------|-----------------|----------------|
| Abstraction Laddering (design thinking) | The mechanics of moving between levels | Treats scope-shifting as a technique, not a developmental journey |
| Moore's Bowling Pin Strategy | The expansion from one niche to adjacent niches | Treats starting narrow as a strategic choice |
| Hormozi's Niche Down | The power of specificity for business traction | Doesn't address WHY some people can't niche |
| Jobs-to-Be-Done Hierarchy | Multiple levels of the same job | Maps job layers, not the person's relationship to them |
| Ansoff Matrix | The risk profile of expanding scope | Purely strategic, no personal development dimension |
| Wilber's Pre/Trans Fallacy | The distinction between pre-rational and trans-rational breadth | Applies to cognitive development, not entrepreneurial scope |
| Kegan's Developmental Theory | How meaning-making develops through stages | Applies to psychology, not problem-scope |
| Palmer's "Let Your Life Speak" | The inward journey of finding vocation | Maps the descent but not the return expansion |
| Plotkin's "Soulcraft" | The descent to specific soul identity | Treats the descent as the destination |

## Open Questions

1. **Multiple Waterfalls:** Can someone have more than one? Or does having multiple mean you're still in The Lake and haven't committed? The Mind Space primary combination suggests one, but users identify with multiple problem categories.

2. **Speed of expansion:** How long should someone stay at The Waterfall before The River develops? Is there a diagnostic for "ready to widen"? Or is the expansion always organic?

3. **Retreat patterns:** What happens when someone reaches The River and experiences a new Crack? Do they fall back to The Lake? Or to a deeper Waterfall? Reality might be more cyclical than the linear model suggests.

4. **Experience Engine thresholds:** After how many experiences should the Scope Map re-run? 3? 5? On demand only? What triggers the "you're moving" notification?

5. **Pre-requisites:** All users have completed /get-started (skills) and problem identification before reaching /create. The diagnostic always has supplementary data. But how heavily should the AI weight existing data vs. the 3 free-text responses?
