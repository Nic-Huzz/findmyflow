# Fantasy League as Spectator Sport

## Vision
Drive to Survive for personal development. The Fantasy League merges reality TV with sport, where the personal transformation journey IS the competition. Participants are simultaneously competing, creating content, and building their brand.

## Spectator Stakes Model
See Obsidian: `Frameworks/Spectator Stakes Model.md`

**Progression:**
1. Identity stakes first (Seasons 1-3): "I am the kind of person who shows up"
2. Soft elimination via promotion/relegation (Season 4+)
3. Financial stakes only if policing is solved (Season 8+)

## Layer 1: Making It Watchable

The app is the **scoring engine and prompt generator**. Social media is the **broadcast layer**. Content lives on the user's platforms, not trapped inside the app.

### Wahoos (High Drama — Already Watchable)
- Before/after emotional state is the money shot
- "I just did something that terrified me" is inherently compelling
- The 3-option self-classification ("Hell yes" / "Felt alive" / "Just did it") IS the dramatic reveal
- Content format: Hook ("I've been avoiding this for 3 years") → Action footage → State reveal ("Was that a Wahoo? Hell yes.")

### Healing (High Vulnerability — Watchable If Framed Right)
- Vulnerability works when self-selected and self-documented (Alone model), not extracted (Lost Resort got destroyed for exploiting trauma)
- Content frame: "Here's what I recognised about myself this week" — the recognition, not the wound
- The Queer Eye principle: empathy-first, never exploitative
- Healing content is what makes this Queer Eye, not Beast Games

### Tune (Low Drama — Made Watchable Through)
- **The streak narrative**: "Day 47 of morning breathwork" has 75 Hard tension — will they break?
- **Drain reveals**: "Here's what's been draining me" is actually high-vulnerability content
- **Stacking format**: 15-second "my Tune today" clip. One isn't watchable. 100 in a row is a transformation story.
- **State transition moments**: Activated → Safe → Vibe Rise across a week is a micro-story
- **Content template**: "Day X. Practice: [what]. State: [before → after]. One sentence." — this is a TikTok format that stacks.

### Live Match Elements
- Live score updates with LIVE badge (already built)
- Momentum graphs showing score progression through the week
- Key plays: specific completions that swung a category
- Match commentary: "Team Firewalkers took Healing early, but a last-day Wahoo surge flipped the result"
- Rivalry seeding: rematches, "last time they met" stats

## Layer 2: Making Players Followable

### Player Cards
- Essence archetype + level + Wahoo style + win record
- Visual identity tied to their hero avatar (already generated via Gemini)
- Stats: W-D-L, favourite category, longest streak, total Wahoos

### Hero's Journey Content Curriculum
Prompts per week give participants a content roadmap alongside their growth curriculum:

**Week 1 — Origin:**
- "Why I joined"
- "What I'm afraid of"
- "My first Wahoo"

**Week 2 — Descent:**
- "What I'm learning about myself"
- "My biggest drain"
- "A healing moment"

**Week 3 — Shift:**
- "Something that shifted"
- "A Wahoo that surprised me"
- "Who I'm becoming"

**Week 4 — Emergence:**
- "Before vs now"
- "What I'd tell myself 4 weeks ago"
- "What's next"

4 weeks × 3 prompts = 12 content pieces minimum. Add daily Tune posts + Wahoo documentation = 30-40 pieces per season. This is a content engine.

### Season Awards
- MVP (highest total RP)
- Comeback Player (biggest week-over-week improvement)
- Most Wahoos (raw courage volume)
- Healing Hero (highest Healing category score)
- Best Content (community-voted)
- Streak King/Queen (longest unbroken daily practice)

### Promotion / Relegation
- Season 1: everyone in Bronze
- Top 2 promote, bottom 2 relegate after each 4-week season
- Tiers: Bronze → Silver → Gold → Diamond
- By Season 3, Diamond league players have battle-tested content and genuine arcs
- Relegation stings without destroying (Duolingo model: "you're in a different league" not "you're out")

## Layer 3: The Broadcast Layer

### Content Template Engine
The app provides the template. The user provides the story and audience. The editing agency provides the polish.

**What the app provides:**
- Weekly prompt ("This week's Wahoo theme: Authority Layer")
- Content format guidance (15s vertical, hook → action → state reveal)
- Scoring incentive (content submission = 2-10 pts in the league)
- Caption/copy templates

**What the user provides:**
- Raw footage of Wahoos, practices, reflections
- Their audience and platform
- Their authentic story

**What the editing agency provides:**
- Professional edits from raw footage
- Consistent brand quality across all participants
- Weekly highlight compilation per player
- Cross-player narrative threads

### Editing Agency Model
- Participants pay an entry fee to compete (covers editing costs + margin)
- Agency receives raw footage from all participants weekly
- Delivers edited content back to each participant for posting
- Also creates the weekly league highlight reel
- Solves the biggest bottleneck: people capture but never edit

### Narrative Structure (Two Layers)
1. **Daily/per-action (user-driven):** Each participant posts their own content daily. They ARE their own narrative. Posts, stories, reels documenting Wahoos, practices, healing moments.
2. **Weekly (Huzz-driven):** Huzz does a weekly review sharing overarching narratives. Match results, upsets, breakthroughs, "Wahoo of the Week," season storylines. This is the DTS narrator layer. Huzz = the commentator.

### Weekly Highlight Reel
- AI-assisted or editor-curated "episode" from the week
- Best Wahoo moments, biggest upsets, key breakthroughs
- Could be: 60s Instagram reel, 5-min YouTube recap, or podcast-style commentary
- Season finale: full awards, before/after compilation, next season preview

### The Flywheel
Content template → User creates post → Post drives engagement on their social → Engagement = brand growth → Brand growth motivates more content → More content = more league points → More points = better matchup → Better matchup = better story → Better story = better content → Vibe Rise grows organically through every participant's audience

### Phase 3: Sponsorship + Scale
- Brand sponsors for league seasons (wellness brands, equipment)
- Top players become ambassadors
- Licensing the format to other communities/niches
- Diamond league content becomes premium spectator content

## Key Design Decisions

1. **Identity stakes first, financial later** — Start with love-motivated participants. Money changes the game and must be introduced only when culture can hold it.
2. **Vulnerability is self-selected** — Users choose what to share. The app provides prompts, not pressure. Alone model, not Lost Resort.
3. **Users own their content** — Posts live on their platforms, not locked in the app. This is the distribution advantage.
4. **The editing agency solves the bottleneck** — Capture is easy. Editing is hard. Remove that friction and content volume explodes.
5. **Two-layer narrative** — Participants tell their story daily. Huzz tells the league story weekly. Both layers reinforce each other.
