# Taxonomy V2 Design Rationale

Date: 2026-04-12
Author: Huzz + Claude

The logic behind every category name, every merge decision, and every placemake choice for both the Skills and Problems wheels.

## Design principles (apply to both wheels)

**1. Recognition over classification.** A burnt-out person scrolling the wheel should pause and think "oh, that's me" without needing to decode an abstract label. Every category name should be a word someone already uses about themselves or the world.

**2. Verb + Placemake structure.** The category name is just a header you scroll past quickly. The placemakes ("Looks like...") do the recognition work. Each placemake names a specific famous person and a concrete moment, so the user can see themselves in someone else's story.

**3. Business angle built in.** Every category has a "turns into" field connecting the play-skill or problem-focus to real business models. Skills without a business angle are hobbies. Problems without a business angle are complaints. The taxonomy connects identity to livelihood.

**4. Evidence-driven, not top-down.** Both taxonomies were built bottom-up: tag 275 people freely, cluster the tags, name the clusters. The current categories are what the evidence produced, not what we designed in advance. If a category exists, it's because 20+ people in the corpus cluster there.

**5. Felt voice, not policy voice.** "Feeling stupid when you're not" beats "Knowledge accessibility gap." "Work that hollows you out" beats "Occupational dignity deficit." The test: would a real person say this to a friend?

## Skills Wheel: 10 categories

### Why 10 (was 12)

The old 12 had two problems:
- **Two dissolved categories** (expressing, influencing) were actually 2-3 different plays stitched together under one label. Expressing was storytelling + performing + truth-telling. Influencing was persuasion + activism + coaching. Splitting them gave cleaner categories.
- **Two overlapping categories** (building + creating, strategizing + organizing) were felt-similar. Building and Creating stayed separate (making physical things vs bringing new things into existence). Strategizing and Organizing merged into Leading (both are about running things).

Net: 12 - 2 dissolved + 1 split (expressing into storytelling + performing) - 1 merge (strategizing + organizing into leading) + 1 new (speaking_up) = 10.

### Category-by-category logic

#### 1. Storytelling
**Why it exists:** "Write something in your actual voice" was the most-used old skill string (27 anchors) but it sprawled across diary writers, protest essayists, and daily-practice writers. These are all storytellers. The category absorbs the narrative-craft side of old `expressing` plus "make meaning out of a mess" from old `synthesizing`.

**Why this name:** People say "I'm a storyteller" or "she's an amazing storyteller." It's the most natural self-descriptor for this play. We tested alternatives (Writing, Narrating, Voicing) but Storytelling is what people actually say.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Turning what you lived into something others can hold" (Frankl) = the personal-experience-to-product pipeline. Memoir, coaching, courses.
2. "Showing up to the page whether you feel like it or not" (Angelou) = the discipline of daily practice. Newsletter, blog, content.
3. "Telling the story the room needs to hear" (Adichie) = story as reframing tool. Keynotes, TED talks, viral content.
4. "Going against the grain of how your world tells stories" (Agassi) = differentiation through honesty. Brand, thought leadership.
5. "Noticing the detail that becomes the whole story" (Ira Glass) = the editorial eye. Added to give the "noticing" play a home here.

#### 2. Teaching
**Why it exists:** Old `clarifying` was already this. Absorbs the "make it accessible" side of old `analyzing` and the framework-building side of old `synthesizing`.

**Why this name:** "Teaching" is universally recognized. "Clarifying" sounded like a corporate competency. Everyone knows what a teacher does.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Making the complex simple enough that anyone can hold it" (Sagan) = scale accessibility. Courses, books, documentaries.
2. "Creating the moment something finally clicks" (Apgar) = the aha-moment engineering. Workshops, tools, demonstrations.
3. "Building a framework others can use without you" (Wooden) = methodology creation. Consulting IP, certifications.
4. "Bridging two worlds that don't know they need each other" (Maathai) = cross-domain translation. Innovation consulting, interdisciplinary work.

#### 3. Coaching
**Why it exists:** Old `nurturing` was already this. Absorbs "move someone from stuck to action" from old `influencing`.

**Why this name:** "Coaching" is what the business is called. "Nurturing" sounded soft and passive. A coach is active, professional, and recognizable as a career.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Holding someone through a hard moment" (Rogers) = emotional safety. Retreats, therapy, holding space.
2. "Showing up with patience until something clicks" (Sullivan) = patient skill-building. 1:1 coaching, tutoring.
3. "Helping someone see what they can't see yet" (Morrison) = talent development. Editorial, mentorship, talent scouting.
4. "Stepping back so someone else can step forward" (Attenborough) = developmental withdrawal. Leadership coaching, succession planning.

#### 4. Performing
**Why it exists:** Split from old `expressing`. The entertainment/stage/keynote half is a completely different play from storytelling. Performing is about being the show. Storytelling is about the craft of narrative. Also absorbs "rally a group" and "make a pitch" from old `influencing`.

**Why this name:** "Performing" is universally recognized. "Expressing" was too vague (expressing what? how?).

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Making the moment itself the message" (Edwin Land) = spectacle as strategy. Event production, launches.
2. "Getting a room to lean forward" (Estee Lauder) = persuasion craft. Sales, fundraising.
3. "Putting yourself on record" (Rita Pierson) = content creation. Podcasts, YouTube, TED.
4. "Performing with nothing but your body and voice" (Miriam Makeba) = embodied performance. Added because the user's personal play includes "Dancing" and physical performance was absent.

#### 5. Creating
**Why it exists:** Old `creating` was already this. Absorbs "make something beautiful and usable" from old `designing` (invention, not refinement).

**Why this name:** Kept the original. "Creating" is the most natural word for "making things that didn't exist."

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Making something that didn't exist before" (Land) = pure invention. Category creation, IP.
2. "Following a hunch everyone said was wrong" (Margulis) = contrarian intuition. R&D, artistic risk.
3. "Remixing two things nobody thought to combine" (Lamarr) = combination innovation. Cross-domain design.
4. "Making something with your hands every single day" (Bourgeois) = daily creative practice. Added because artists/musicians/chefs were underrepresented.

#### 6. Building
**Why it exists:** Old `building` was already this. Distinct from Creating because Building is about execution and materials, not invention.

**Why this name:** Kept the original. "Building" is what makers call themselves.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Getting your hands on the actual thing" (Ferrari) = hands-on craft. Physical products, hardware.
2. "Prototyping until it works no matter how many tries" (Dyson) = obsessive iteration. R&D, product development.
3. "Doing whatever it takes to physically bring it into existence" (Graves) = sweat-equity bootstrapping. Maker businesses.
4. "Building something from scratch because nothing good enough exists" (Lutke) = digital building. Added because all original examples were physical.
5. "Noticing what's broken and not being able to leave it alone" (Ford) = the noticing-as-building play. Added to give the analyzing-flavored tags a home here.

#### 7. Designing
**Why it exists:** Old `designing` minus the invention cases (which moved to Creating). Pure aesthetic/experience/feel work.

**Why this name:** Kept the original. "Designing" is what designers call themselves.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Shaping how a space or moment feels" (Disney) = experience design. Events, hospitality, interiors.
2. "Obsessing over the detail everyone else skips" (Kurosawa) = micro-craft. Luxury, artisan, premium.
3. "Stripping away everything that doesn't belong" (Chanel) = radical simplification. UX, brand.

Only 3 because the evidence only supported 3 genuinely distinct design plays. A 4th ("taste/knowing what's good before others") was considered but only had 2 clean anchors (Jobs, Chanel) both used elsewhere.

#### 8. Leading
**Why it exists:** Merged old `strategizing` + old `organizing`. Both are "running things" from different angles. Strategy = designing the game. Operations = making the machine run.

**Why this name:** "Leading" covers both strategy and operations. "Strategizing" excluded the operations people. "Organizing" excluded the strategists.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Seeing a future nobody else sees and building the rules to get there" (Sol Price) = visionary strategy. Sharpened from the original "Deciding what matters" to add the visionary dimension that was flagged as missing.
2. "Building a system that runs without you in the room" (Bezos) = operational excellence. Systems, processes, leadership principles.
3. "Making the right call when the stakes are highest" (Thorp) = high-pressure decision-making. Crisis management, executive advisory.
4. "Turning a mess no one wants to touch into something that runs" (Rustin) = turnaround operations. The March on Washington in 8 weeks.
5. "Spotting the number everyone else walked past" (Rockefeller) = the noticing-as-leading play. Added to give analyzing-flavored tags a home in Leading.

#### 9. Connecting
**Why it exists:** Old `connecting` was already this. No change except absorbing the federation/coordination cases from old `organizing`.

**Why this name:** Kept the original. "Connecting" is what connectors call themselves.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Building a community from nothing" (Franklin) = community creation. Memberships, networks.
2. "Holding space for a conversation nobody else will host" (Oprah) = facilitation. Group coaching, mediation.
3. "Making a gathering people walk away changed by" (Addams) = event production. Retreats, conferences.
4. "Being the person who holds a scattered room together" (Trevor Noah) = team glue. Partnership brokering, team consulting.

#### 10. Speaking up
**Why it exists:** Split from old `expressing` (the truth-telling/courage half) + old `influencing` (the body-as-argument, change-minds-through-conviction half). These are NOT performing. Performing is about the show. Speaking up is about the risk.

**Why this name:** "Speaking up" is what people say when they describe courage in everyday contexts. "I need to speak up." "She spoke up when nobody else would." Tested alternatives (Truth-telling, Witnessing, Advocating) but Speaking up is the most commonly used.

**Placemakes logic:**
1. "Speaking truth to power face to face" (Fannie Lou Hamer) = direct confrontation. Advocacy, lobbying.
2. "Speaking from inside your own wound" (Audre Lorde) = vulnerability as platform. Memoir-as-movement, brand.
3. "Standing alone against an industry that rewards silence" (Politkovskaya) = investigative courage. Journalism, whistleblowing.
4. "Refusing to stop saying it when no one's listening yet" (Maathai) = long-arc persistence. Movement building, cause entrepreneurship.
5. "Challenging the room you have to keep working in tomorrow" (Marva Collins) = everyday institutional courage. Added because the other 4 are famous public figures, but FindMyFlow's audience is burnt-out professionals who stopped speaking up at work, not activists on world stages.

## Problems Wheel: 12 categories

### Why 12 (was 12, all different)

The number stayed the same but every category changed. The old 12 were policy categories (physical_vitality, social_justice, human_progress). The new 12 are felt wounds derived from 524 free-form problem tags across 275 people.

The initial bottom-up clustering produced 13 categories. After review:
- **Identity stolen or forced** (70% confidence) merged into **The voice that got taken** because identity erasure IS a form of silencing.
- **Systems that don't see you** + **People falling through the cracks** merged into **Being left behind** because both are about being abandoned by structures.
- **Knowledge locked away** split into **Feeling stupid when you're not** (jargon/complexity) and **Locked out of what you need** (cost/access/credentials) because these are felt-different problems.

Net: 13 - 1 merge (identity into voice) - 1 merge (systems + cracks into left behind) + 1 split (knowledge into feeling stupid + locked out) = 12.

### Category-by-category logic

#### 1. Kids who deserved better
**Why it exists:** 42 tags clustered around adults failing children. Montessori, Korczak, Rogers, Paley, Pierson, Escalante, Anne Sullivan all worked on this.

**Why this name:** "Kids who deserved better" is what someone says when they're angry about how children are treated. It's personal, warm, and sad. Not "child development" (clinical) or "youth advocacy" (policy).

**Key decision:** Including Agassi (forced into tennis by his father). His problem is specific and personal, but it resonates with anyone whose parents chose their life for them.

#### 2. The voice that got taken
**Why it exists:** 78 tags (largest category after absorbing Identity). The core wound: someone was prevented from saying what they see, feel, or know. Covers voice suppression, identity erasure, gender silencing, censorship.

**Why this name:** "The voice that got taken" is visceral and passive. The voice was TAKEN. It wasn't lost. Someone took it.

**Key decision:** Absorbing "Identity stolen or forced" here rather than keeping it separate. Evidence: Lorde's identity work IS her voice work. Adichie's "single story" IS about who gets to define you. The distinction between "voice taken" and "identity stolen" was conceptual, not felt.

#### 3. Pain that nobody takes seriously
**Why it exists:** 24 tags about physical suffering being ignored or dismissed. Small but fiercely distinct.

**Why this name:** The problem isn't just pain. It's pain that nobody believes, names, or takes seriously. The dismissal IS the wound. Kahlo's doctors, Saunders's hospitals, Farmer's global health system.

**Key decision:** Including Huffington's burnout collapse. Burnout is contested as "real pain" in some frameworks. But for FindMyFlow's audience, burnout IS the body saying what the mind won't. It belongs here.

#### 4. The world we're losing
**Why it exists:** 23 tags (smallest). Ecological destruction, species loss, climate crisis. Tight, clean, no overlaps.

**Why this name:** "The world we're losing" is elegiac. Not angry, not activist. Sad. It names the feeling of watching something beautiful disappear.

**Key decision:** Including Kimmerer's language argument ("bay is a verb in Potawatomi"). This is the most intellectually unusual placemake in the whole taxonomy. It shifts the problem from "we're destroying nature" to "our language makes it impossible to treat nature as alive." That reframe is why Kimmerer is here and not in Teaching.

#### 5. A life that isn't yours to live
**Why it exists:** 35 tags about systems of control: slavery, colonialism, apartheid, authoritarianism. The wound: someone else decides your life.

**Why this name:** "A life that isn't yours to live" is felt from the inside. Not "oppression" (academic) or "social justice" (policy). The person experiencing it would say: "this life isn't mine."

**Key decision:** Gender-oppression tags moved to "The voice that got taken" because gender oppression most commonly manifests as silencing. "A life that isn't yours" focuses on institutional/structural control: governments, laws, economic systems.

#### 6. Feeling stupid when you're not
**Why it exists:** Split from the larger "Knowledge locked away" cluster. The jargon/complexity half. 48 tags.

**Why this name:** The strongest name in the entire taxonomy. "Feeling stupid when you're not" names the exact felt experience of being excluded by unnecessary complexity. Nobody says "I care about knowledge accessibility." Everyone has felt stupid when they weren't.

**Key decision:** Splitting this from "Locked out." Someone who can't afford education (locked out) and someone who reads a medical leaflet and blames themselves for not understanding (feeling stupid) are experiencing different wounds. One is about access. The other is about self-blame induced by bad communication.

#### 7. Locked out of what you need
**Why it exists:** Split from "Knowledge locked away." The cost/credentials/gatekeeping half. 43 tags.

**Why this name:** "Locked out" is visceral. You can see the door. You can see what's behind it. You just can't get in.

**Key decision:** Including Carnegie (libraries). His problem was poverty-as-gatekeeping. He saw that a single free library changed his entire life trajectory and then built 2,509 of them. That's "locked out" made personal.

#### 8. Your work being treated as nothing
**Why it exists:** 76 tags about creative work being dismissed, stolen, ignored, or blocked. Originally "Creative work dismissed," renamed to be more personal.

**Why this name:** "Your work being treated as nothing" speaks directly to the person. Not "creative work" in the abstract. YOUR work. The one you made.

**Key decision:** Including Tesla's credit theft alongside af Klint's hidden paintings. These are different surfaces of the same wound: you made something real and the world acted like it didn't exist.

#### 9. Being left behind
**Why it exists:** Merged "Systems that don't see you" (structural blindness) + "People falling through the cracks" (abandonment). 47 tags combined.

**Why this name:** "Being left behind" is what someone says when they look around and realize nobody came back for them.

**Key decision:** Merging two clusters that were conceptually distinct (system design vs human consequences) but felt-similar. A user who cares about "systems built without me" also cares about "people falling through the cracks." The merge lost a subtle distinction but gained a stronger, clearer category.

#### 10. Forgetting what it was all for
**Why it exists:** 31 tags about meaninglessness, purpose-loss, existential emptiness. FindMyFlow's core audience problem.

**Why this name:** "Forgetting what it was all for" is the exact sentence a burnt-out person says at 2am. Not "existential crisis" (clinical). Not "meaning deficit" (academic). The simple, sad statement: I forgot.

**Key decision:** This is the category FindMyFlow exists to serve. Every user who downloads the app is, in some form, here. The placemakes (Frankl, Campbell, Aurelius, Wooden) are deliberately diverse across eras and domains to show that this isn't a modern problem. It's the oldest human question.

#### 11. People who stopped wondering
**Why it exists:** 50 tags about the damage done by certainty, dogma, and closed minds. The most intellectually surprising category. Nobody would predict it, but it's unmistakable in the data.

**Why this name:** "People who stopped wondering" is gentler than "certainty as poison" (the working name). It frames the problem as a loss (they stopped wondering) rather than an accusation (they're poisonous). More inviting for a user who recognizes this in their own workplace.

**Key decision:** Keeping this as its own category rather than merging into "Feeling stupid when you're not." Both involve knowledge being distorted, but "feeling stupid" is about being excluded BY experts. "Stopped wondering" is about experts who stopped being curious themselves. Different wound, different placemakes.

#### 12. Work that hollows you out
**Why it exists:** 29 tags about labor exploitation, dignity-stripping jobs, and burnout-inducing systems. FindMyFlow's second core audience problem (alongside "Forgetting what it was all for").

**Why this name:** "Work that hollows you out" names what burnout actually feels like. Not exhaustion (that's a symptom). Hollowing. The slow removal of everything that made you who you were.

**Key decision:** The Cucinelli placemake ("Watching your father come home from work with his dignity gone") is the strongest single placemake in the entire taxonomy. It was chosen as the lead because it shows the problem through a child's eyes, which makes it universal. Everyone has seen someone they love broken by work.

## The "Noticing" decision

Early in the skills design, we considered an 11th skill category called "Noticing" (detective work, pattern-finding, data analysis). It was cut because noticing is supplementary, not primary. Nobody builds a business around "I notice things." They build it around what they DO with what they notice: teach it, lead with it, write about it.

Instead of a separate Noticing category, we added noticing-flavored placemakes to the categories where the noticing leads to action:
- Storytelling: "Noticing the detail that becomes the whole story" (Ira Glass)
- Building: "Noticing what's broken and not being able to leave it alone" (Henry Ford)
- Leading: "Spotting the number everyone else walked past" (Rockefeller)

The old `analyzing` tags were re-routed to each person's primary play category using smart routing: a data analyst who mainly teaches goes to Teaching; one who mainly leads goes to Leading.

## What we tested and rejected

- **"Facilitating" as a category:** The user's personal mental model includes "Facilitating" but it cuts across multiple categories (Teaching + Connecting + Coaching). Keeping it as a compound rather than a category preserves cleaner boundaries.
- **"Tending" as a category:** Captures "returning to the same thing for decades" (Goodall, McClintock, Murakami). Cut because it's supplementary like Noticing. The long-arc discipline lives as a flavor within whichever category the person's discipline serves.
- **"Witnessing" as a category:** Early name for what became "Speaking up." Cut because "witnessing" has religious connotations and is less universally recognized. "Speaking up" is what people actually say.
- **Matching skills count to problems count:** Skills has 10, Problems has 12. We considered forcing both to 10 but the evidence showed problems need more categories because suffering comes in more forms than play does.
