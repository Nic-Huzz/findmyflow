# P3: Jobbers, coordinators, organisers and asset owners

- Uncovering the forces that make an approach work (or not work)…

Thanks for the hard work and generous insights on Lesson 1 and Lesson 2. In this lesson, we cover some of the same ground, but go a lot deeper.

The goal is to help you uncover the forces that make an approach work (or not work) in these sorts of projects.

One you see them, they’re difficult to un-see.

Before we go to the videos, **here’s a bonus from Liz Gilbert**. Seth filmed this interview with her as a course for Acumen, and it brings up a core message we’re trying to get to as we explore bootstrapping.

In short: if you do it right, it’s a career. A great job. A way to do work that you’re proud of. But don’t confuse it with your vocation, your art, your purpose in the world. Sometimes they align, but they usually don’t.

In this workshop, at least for now, act ‘as if’. What if you could merely (simply) create value for people who need you to create value, to do it as a professional, to do it with care. It doesn’t have to be personal. And it doesn’t have to be something you’d buy from you. It can simply be something that’s useful, irresistible and profitable…

**Video notes:**

- Hobby, job, career, vocation - these are four different words because they're four different things
    - Hobby: things you do for pleasure - the stakes are 0
    - Job: You need this - contract you have with society to live (pay for things) - you don’t need to love it - it's something you do for money

    - Vocation: A calling - the highest possible pursuit you can do and no one can take it from

**Lesson 3 Summary**

**Jobbers**

- Take something a factory makes, and offer it to a select audience in a way that’s more delightful, more efficient or more effective than they can do without you.
- This sounds simple, and it’s tempting to build a very thin wall of difference. It seems easy to buy a product at wholesale, build a website to sell it, mark the price up and create some sort of “passive” income. But that almost never happens because anyone can do that, so they will, and the price war will begin.
- Instead, the objective here is that you can do this effectively if you are SPECIFIC, if you are content with small and focused instead of large and generic.

**Coordinators**

- Generate value by connecting people together. Common examples are a conference like TED, a directory, and a job fair.
- If it was only one person or one group, it wouldn’t work.

**Labor organizer**

- If you know the labor and the labor trusts you, you are providing a huge service to them. And, if you know the person who needs the labor and they trust you, you are providing a huge service to them.
- The hard part is finding someone who needs the labor bad enough but isn’t willing to go to all the steps to coordinate the labor.

**Asset Owner**

- You own an asset, a device, or a thing that makes life easier for the people who use it.
- Either they use it by renting it or because you use the asset for them.
