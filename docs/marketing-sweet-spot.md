# Marketing Sweet Spot
*Trust x Attention. Original IP: Huzz Hurrell / FindMyFlow. April 2026.*

---

## The Insight

Seth Godin: Marketing = Trust + Attention.

Every experience creator who became internationally known nailed both. Trust through repetition (showing up consistently for years). Attention through being remarkable (literally worth remarking on).

Most experience creators are in "Kept Secret." They've been doing the work. They have trust from the people who've found them. But nobody is talking about them because nothing about their approach is remarkable enough to remark on.

The Marketing Sweet Spot is where trust and attention grow together. Not attention without trust (flash in the pan). Not trust without attention (best kept secret). Both, proportionally.

### The Four Zones

**Hidden** (low trust + low attention): Just starting. No track record, no one talking about you. Everyone starts here. The work is: start showing up (trust) and find your remarkable angle (attention).

**Flash** (low trust + high attention): Got noticed but haven't earned trust yet. A viral post, a bold claim, a striking debut. Dangerous if you can't back it up. The work is: keep showing up until the attention converts to credibility.

**Kept Secret** (high trust + low attention): This is where most FindMyFlow users are. You've been doing the work. Your clients love you. But nobody outside your small circle knows you exist. The work is: find what's remarkable about what you already do.

**Marketing Sweet Spot** (high trust + high attention): Trusted AND talked about. The diagonal. Brene Brown, Wim Hof, Priya Parker. They didn't get here by accident. Trust came from years of repetition. Attention came from being remarkable.

## What Makes Someone Remarkable

Analyzed 59 internationally known experience creators. Seven natural categories emerged:

### 1. Authority Paradox
*Gained credibility by lacking traditional qualifications or actively rejecting expertise.*

The remarkable thing is the gap between who they are and what they teach. A bartender teaching business. A yoga teacher who never corrects form. A bankrupt person teaching finance. The paradox IS the marketing.

**Creators:** Adriene Mishler, Dave Ramsey, Gabby Bernstein, Marie Forleo, Marshall Ganz, Tiffany Aliche

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What would surprise people about your background relative to what you teach?"

### 2. Opposite Method
*Succeeded by doing the exact opposite of established practice in their field.*

Everyone in the field does it one way. They do the opposite. And it works. The contrast with the norm IS the remarkable thing.

**Creators:** Alain de Botton, Dan Hughes, David Bohm, Gabor Mate, Harville Hendrix, James Clear, Phil Jackson, Stan Tatkin, Terry Real

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What does everyone in your field do that you think is wrong? What do you do instead?"

### 3. Breaking Boundaries
*Made private things public or refused to stay within expected categories.*

They said the thing you're not supposed to say. They crossed the line between personal and professional. They brought something from behind closed doors into the open.

**Creators:** Esther Perel, Glennon Doyle, Holly Whitaker, Jay Shetty, Kae Tempest, Lin-Manuel Miranda

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What do you know from lived experience that your field pretends doesn't exist?"

### 4. Impossible Proof
*Demonstrated that something everyone knew couldn't work actually could.*

The remarkable thing is the evidence. Not a claim. Not a theory. Proof that the impossible works. Data that silenced skeptics.

**Creators:** Bill Thomas, John Gottman, Marva Collins, Sue Johnson, Wim Hof

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What result do you consistently get that people would call impossible or unlikely?"

### 5. Power from Failure
*Transformed personal catastrophe or rejection into their source of authority.*

The wound became the credential. The failure became the qualification. The thing that should have ended their career started it.

**Creators:** Bill Wilson, Nina Simone, Paulo Freire, Václav Havel

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What's the worst thing that happened to you that gave you something nobody else has?"

### 6. Invisible Impact
*Chose anonymity or behind-the-scenes work over personal recognition.*

The remarkable thing is the refusal to be the face. They built movements, not personal brands. The impact was visible. They weren't.

**Creators:** Ella Baker, Grace Lee Boggs, Jane Addams, Tarana Burke

**Diagnostic question for users:** "Would you rather build something that outlasts you or something that features you?"

### 7. Commitment as Art
*The sheer duration or intensity of dedication became the remarkable thing.*

They didn't do anything flashy. They just did the same thing for so long, with such intensity, that the commitment itself became extraordinary.

**Creators:** Joseph Campbell, Mercedes Sosa, Rita Pierson, Tenzin Palmo, Yayoi Kusama

**Diagnostic question for users:** "What have you been doing for so long that the duration itself is the story?"

## UPDATED: The 5 Remarkable Triggers (v3)

*Replaces the 7 categories above. Based on analysis of 57 creators.*

The original 7 categories were discovered through clustering. The refined model is based on WHY HUMANS REMARK, not what creators did. Five cognitive triggers that make someone say "wait, what?" and tell a friend.

### The 5 Triggers

1. **Rule Broken** (95% of creators) — Challenged a cultural standard or norm. The most universal trigger.
2. **Unexpected Combination** (77%) — Two domains brought together that the field hasn't seen combined.
3. **Extreme Action** (53%) — A specific visible behavior so far beyond normal it becomes the thing people talk about. NOT the same as extreme commitment (which is the trust axis).
4. **Stupid Simplicity** (77%) — A conclusion so simple it feels insulting, arrived at through years of depth.
5. **Impossible Result** (67%) — Evidence that defies what the field believed was possible.

### Key distinction: Extreme Commitment vs Extreme Action

- **Extreme commitment** = showing up consistently for years. This is the TRUST axis. Non-negotiable. Every creator has this (average 12 years before inflection).
- **Extreme action** = a specific VISIBLE behavior that makes people say "wait, what?" This is a remarkability trigger. Only 53% have it. Present when it IS the rule break made visible.

### Three Patterns of Rule Breaking

| Pattern | Count | % | Description |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Reframing** | 25 | 46% | Ask a question that makes the old rule look absurd. "Why the pain, not the addiction?" |
| **Additive** | 17 | 31% | Do something nobody in the field does. Wim Hof adding ice baths, Tony Robbins adding fire walking. |
| **Subtractive** | 12 | 22% | Refuse something everyone assumes is required. Adriene Mishler never correcting form. |

**Reframing is the most common and most accessible path.** Nearly half of all remarkable creators became remarkable by asking a single question.

### Extreme Action ↔ Rule Breaking Connection

When extreme action exists, it almost always serves the rule break:

| Connection | Count | Description |
|---|---|---|
| Inseparable | 19 | The practice IS the norm violation. Every repetition breaks the rule. (Wim Hof, Myles Horton) |
| Sequential | 11 | Extreme experience happened first, rule break emerged later. (Gabor Mate, Jay Shetty) |
| Evidence | 9 | The extreme action proves the rule wrong through results. (John Gottman, Phil Jackson) |
| Separate | 9 | Rule break exists without extreme action. (Marie Forleo, James Clear) |
| Same moment | 7 | One event is both audacious and norm-violating. (Glennon Doyle, Walt Disney) |

### The Craft Stays Constant

Across all 57 creators analyzed: the CRAFT (the thing they do) stays constant. What changes is the CHANNEL (how it reaches people). Brene Brown always researched shame. The channel went from papers to TED to Netflix. Wim Hof always practiced cold + breathing. The audience went from nobody to scientists to the public.

**Implication for users: stop looking for a new thing. Keep doing YOUR thing. What needs to change is how it reaches people, not what you do.**

### The Rule Break Creates the Tribe

The rule break is what creates distribution. People who agree with the broken rule become the marketing engine. Sharing the rule break is an act of identity: "I believe this too."

- Brene Brown's tribe: people who believe vulnerability isn't weakness
- Wim Hof's tribe: people who believe the body knows more than medicine admits
- Holly Whitaker's tribe: women who think AA doesn't speak for them
- Adriene Mishler's tribe: people who believe yoga doesn't need a studio

**The tribe IS the distribution. You don't need a platform if the rule you broke is one that enough people secretly wanted broken.**

The diagnostic question for users: **"What do the people you serve already believe but nobody in your field is saying?"**

### How to find your rule break

The rule that caused your burnout is often the rule you're most qualified to challenge. The protective archetype IS the internalized rule:

| Archetype | The rule they internalized | The rule to break externally |
|---|---|---|
| Performer | "Your worth equals your output" | Transformation doesn't require hustle |
| Perfectionist | "You need to be ready first" | You can help people before you've figured yourself out |
| Ghost | "Stay invisible until you're safe" | Being seen IS the work |
| Controller | "Stay in control or everything falls apart" | The best results come from letting go |

### 6 Sources of Rule Breaks (with Life Map data)

Users complete a Life Map covering 5 life periods (childhood, teens, young adult, career, now) with skills, problems, and personas per period. This data directly feeds the rule-break diagnostic.

**Source 1: Your wound reveals the rule**
The thing that hurt you IS the rule that's wrong.

*Diagnostic question:* "What system failed you? That's the rule."

*Life Map connection:* The **problems** entries across all 5 periods surface the wounds. The pattern across periods reveals the rule. If "not fitting in" appears in childhood AND "feeling like the odd one out at work" appears in career, the rule is: "You need to conform to belong." The break: "You don't. Your weirdness is the product."

*Example:* Gabor Mate's wound (his own ADHD + childhood trauma) revealed that "treat the symptom" was the wrong rule.

---

**Source 2: Import from outside your field**
Bring something from a domain nobody in your field has touched.

*Diagnostic question:* "What do you know from a completely different world that nobody in your field has tried?"

*Life Map connection:* The **skills** entries across periods show what domains the user has lived in. A user who coded in their teens, studied psychology in young adulthood, and ran workshops in their career has THREE domains. The combination is unique. The import: "What if we applied coding logic to workshop design?" or "What if we used psychological frameworks to teach programming?"

*Example:* Phil Jackson imported Zen Buddhism (from personal practice) into NBA coaching (his profession). Two domains from different life periods.

---

**Source 3: Watch what actually works**
Observe reality. The gap between what the field SAYS and what actually WORKS is the rule to break.

*Diagnostic question:* "What have you seen work that your field says shouldn't?"

*Life Map connection:* The **career** and **now** period entries capture what energized the user at work and what they want to change. The gap between "what energized me" and "what my field taught me to do" IS the observation. If they were energized by informal conversations but their field values formal presentations, the rule to break is about formality.

*Example:* Adriene Mishler observed that people practiced more yoga when nobody corrected their form. Her field said correction was essential.

---

**Source 4: Your biography disproves the rule**
Your life story IS the argument. You don't need research. You need to tell the truth.

*Diagnostic question:* "What would surprise people about your background relative to what you do now?"

*Life Map connection:* The FULL life map is the biography. The contrast between periods IS the remarkable story. A user who struggled with identity in their teens, burned out in corporate in their career, and now runs healing events has a biography that disproves "you need to have it together to help others." The Life Map shows the journey from wound to work.

*Example:* Marie Forleo's biography (bartender → business teacher) disproves "you need credentials to teach."

---

**Source 5: Do the opposite**
Flip the standard practice. If everyone in your field does X, do not-X.

*Diagnostic question:* "What does everyone in your field do that you think is backwards?"

*Life Map connection:* The **problems** entries in the career period capture frustrations with how things are done. "People stuck in jobs they hate" implies the field's approach to career guidance is broken. The opposite of the field's standard is often visible in the user's own instincts: what they naturally do instead when nobody is watching.

*Example:* Phil Jackson: coaches yell → he sat in silence. Dan Hughes: discipline raging kids → he stayed curious.

---

**Source 6: Ask "why not?"**
Question an accepted limitation. The answer is usually "because we've always done it this way."

*Diagnostic question:* "What limitation in your field does everyone accept that you think is absurd?"

*Life Map connection:* The **now** period asks "What do you want to change?" The answer often contains an implicit "why not?" If a user writes "the way mental health is treated" as what they want to change, the follow-up is: "What specifically about it? What would you do differently?" That difference IS the "why not?"

*Example:* Holly Whitaker: "Why is a programme designed by men in 1935 still the only option for recovery?"

Recurring wound: "Real transformation is paywalled." "The healing industry diagnoses instead of explores." "Serious or woo-woo framing makes people cringe."
The rule: Healing requires credentials, money, seriousness, and a clinical setting.
The break: Healing works better when it's fun, accessible, and communal.

**Source 2 (import from outside):**
Domains from Life Map: VC/finance + tech product building + breathwork facilitation + framework design + comedy/performance energy.

**Source 3 (what actually works vs what the field says):**
Field says: individual therapy, serious framing, clinical language.
What the data shows works: "Compressing a 45-minute flow journey into a 3-minute performance." "Walking in with energy and turning audiences into participants within minutes." Group transformation through play, not analysis.

**Source 4 (biography disproves rule):**
"Serious or woo-woo framing makes people cringe, including me, five years ago." The user WAS the person who wouldn't have touched healing. Biography (VC analyst, $30K on 52 courses, burned out, found healing through play) disproves: "healing is only for woo-woo people."

**Source 5 (do the opposite):**
Field standard: diagnose, treat, serious, expensive, individual.

**Source 6 (why not?):**
"Why does transformation require a therapist's office when the best healing I've experienced happened dancing with strangers?"

### Assessment

The Life Map data contains everything needed for rule-break discovery. The framework reads the same data through a different lens: not "what are your skills" but "what rule do your skills exist to break." Not "what problems do you care about" but "what system failed you."

The output is directionally right but not perfect. Refinement comes from the user testing the rule break with real audiences and seeing what creates the tribe response ("FINALLY, someone said it"). The framework provides the starting point. The experience engine (creating and running events) provides the validation.
