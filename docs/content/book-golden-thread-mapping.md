### The Three-Act Narrative Spine

These three statements form the narrative arc of the entire book:

1. **The Installation:** We are born as love (essence), into a culture that installs fear (conditioning), through a school system that breaks the software (micro-traumas).
2. **The Wound:** The body stores these wounds (nervous system). Our frequency drops. We chase external fixes that never change the internal.
3. **The Return:** Healing = restoring the river's flow. Safety + Expression = Vibe Rise. The mission: make people safe to return to themselves.

```
CURIOSITY (mind)      → You already know what you want.
NERVOUS SYSTEM (body) → Your body won't let you go get it.
EXPERIENCE (life)     → When the cone expands, curiosity becomes lived experience.
                        Purpose emerges FROM experiences, not before them.
```

## MasterMind Council Review (2026-06-24)

*Council members: Ken Wilber (integral mapping), Paulo Coelho (narrative soul), Naval Ravikant (ruthless simplification)*

### Wilber — The Integral Cartographer

The 9 branches operate at **mixed levels of abstraction**. Some are ontological claims about reality ("experience is all there is"), some are developmental observations ("culture installs fear"), some are prescriptive ("healing = return to flow"), and one is a mission statement. This is the real problem, not the count.

**Wilber's proposed restructuring (3 layers, not 9 branches):**

| Layer | What it is | Branches |
|-------|-----------|----------|
| **Ontological** (what IS) | The ground. The lens. Not chapters. | Experience is all there is. Everything is frequency. We are vessels. |
| **Developmental** (what HAPPENED) | The narrative arc. These ARE your chapters. | Essence → Installation → Protection → Safety ceiling |
| **Transformational** (what HEALS) | The method. | 4 R's, the river, the groan, dome expansion |

**On the 17 flagged chapters:** They're **transitional chapters** sitting at boundaries between layers. They're the most important chapters in the book precisely because they refuse to sit neatly in one box. Stop trying to assign them. Mark them as threshold moments.

**On "The Groan" as missing branch:** It's not a branch. It's the **mechanism of transition** between the developmental and transformational layers. It's what moves you from understanding a stage to actually transitioning through it.

**On "The Flood":** Adequate but passive. 'The Seeing' or 'The Mirror' would be more accurate, but 'The Flood' has emotional resonance. Keep it.

### Coelho — The Alchemist

**On title:** Must be **Find Your Flow**, not Vibe Rise. 'Vibe Rise' is your language, your framework, your app. It means nothing to someone who hasn't entered your world yet. 'Find Your Flow' is a sentence the reader already has inside them. Vibe Rise is what they discover inside the book. Find Your Flow is what makes them open it.

When The Alchemist's publisher wanted to call it "The Personal Legend," Coelho refused. The title names the transformation, not the destination.

**On the Golden Thread's visibility:** **Invisible in the text. Visible only in the epigraphs.**

The raw thought entries as epigraphs — dated, unpolished, real — let the reader witness the seed before the flower. The moment you write "this book is built on 9 branches of understanding," you've written a textbook, not a story. The reader should finish the book and feel the worldview has entered them without ever having it explained.

**On the book → app journey:** The reader finishes Find Your Flow, opens the app, discovers it's called Vibe Rise — and now that name means something. The book teaches them the language. The app is where they speak it.

### Naval — The Razor

**On branches as organisational framework:** Throw them out. The book already has a perfect structure: three parts, three models, three questions. The branches are interesting for YOU (how your worldview developed). They're not interesting for the READER. The reader cares about one thing: "Am I somewhere in this story?"

**Where branches ARE useful:** The epigraph idea. Use them to select the strongest seed-thought for each chapter, then put them away.

**On all 6 questions:**
- 9 vs 6+3: Neither. Branches are a research tool, not a book structure.
- 17 flagged chapters: They're flagged because the best chapters resist categorisation. Stop trying to box them.
- The Groan as missing branch: No. It's the entire thesis of Part 3. It's bigger than a branch.
- Title: Find Your Flow. The title that requires zero explanation wins.
- The Flood: It works. Don't overthink naming.
- **Start writing.** 92% confidence structure + 196 seeds = diminishing returns on analysis.
