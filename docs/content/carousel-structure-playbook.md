# Carousel Structure Playbook

## The Algorithm Facts (2026)

- **Slide 3 is the checkpoint.** Instagram tracks swipe-through to slide 3+. Clear the average → re-serve queue (shown again, including to non-followers).
- **60%+ completion rate** → Explore page distribution.
- **Saves and shares** outweigh likes. DM shares weighted 3-5x higher than likes.
- **Carousels dominate**: 3.1-5.8% engagement vs 1.8-3.5% Reels vs 1.2-2.0% single images. 2x more saves than Reels.

## Drop-Off Data

- **Slide 1→2**: Hook failed if below 50% swipe-through
- **Slide 2→3**: Promise didn't deliver. Bridge between hook and payoff broke.
- **Slide 4-5**: Need a contrarian/surprising element to spike completion
- **12+ slides**: 40% drop-off unless highly serialized
- **Optimal length**: 7-10 slides
- **Text per slide**: 15-30 words (max ~25). One idea per slide.

## Slide 1 Hook Formulas

1. **Specific number + claim**: "7 caption hooks that doubled our reply rate"
2. **Relatable pain**: "You're doing the work but nothing's changing"
3. **Surprising stat**: "93% of buying decisions start with a visual impression"
4. **Direct question**: "Want to know why your carousel posts aren't converting?"
5. **Bold contrarian**: "Everything you know about X is wrong"
6. **Before with no after**: Shows a state that demands resolution (open loop)
7. **A number that seems wrong**: Cognitive dissonance resolved only by swiping

**Draft hook slide LAST** — once value is locked, you know what promise to make.

## The 8 Carousel Frameworks (Reference)

### 1. AIDA (Attention-Interest-Desire-Action)
| Slides | Stage | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 1-2 | Attention | Bold claim, specific promise, relatable pain |
| 3-5 | Interest | Name problem, add context, data, counter-intuitive insight |
| 6-7 | Desire | Paint outcome, social proof, remove objections |
| 8-10 | Action | Single clear CTA. Save CTA drives algo reach. |

### 2. PAS (Problem-Agitate-Solve)
| Slides | Stage | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Hook | Name the problem in their words |
| 2-3 | Problem | Explore it, show it's real and widespread |
| 4-5 | Agitate | Consequences, frustration, cost of leaving unsolved |
| 6-7 | Solve | Your solution as the obvious resolution |
| 8 | CTA | Action tied to solution |

### 3. 5-Slide Story Arc
| Slides | Beat | Content |
|--------|------|---------|
| 1 | Hook | Problem, bold claim, or visual interrupt |
| 2-3 | Tension | "Before" state, friction points |
| 4-5 | Turn | Method/product as resolution |
| 6-8 | Proof | Social proof, before/after, context |
| 9-10 | Close | CTA, urgency, reinforcement |

### 4. Hero's Journey Carousel
| Slide | Stage | Content |
|-------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Ordinary World | Current pain / starting situation |
| 2 | Call to Adventure | Catalyst moment |
| 3 | Challenges | Obstacles encountered |
| 4 | Transformation | Positive change |
| 5 | Return | New reality achieved |

### 5. Before-After-Bridge (BAB)
| Slides | Stage | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 1-2 | Before | "I used to..." Paint current situation |
| 3-4 | After | "Now I..." Vivid contrast |
| 5-7 | Bridge | How transformation happened (method lives here) |
| 8 | CTA | This is available to you too |

### 6. Myth-Busting
| Slides | Stage | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Myth | State it boldly. Cognitive dissonance. |
| 2-4 | Correction | Progressive revelation. "But here's the truth..." |
| 5-6 | Implication | What this means for them |
| 7 | CTA | Share/comment |

### 7. Contrarian Hot Take
| Slide | Content |
|-------|---------|
| 1 | Provocative statement |
| 2 | Why most people believe the opposite |
| 3-6 | Evidence for your position |
| 7 | Actionable alternative |
| 8 | CTA (comment-based, polarisation drives comments) |

### 8. Personal Journey / Story-Lesson
| Slides | Stage | Content |
|--------|-------|---------|
| 1 | Hook | "The mistake that taught me everything about X" |
| 2 | Context | Where you were. What you believed. |
| 3-5 | Struggle | What happened. The mess. One beat per slide. |
| 6-7 | Breakthrough | Insight emerges FROM the story |
| 8 | Application | What you do differently now |
| 9 | Takeaway | One-line lesson, generalised to them |
| 10 | CTA | Save, share, comment their version |

## Huzz's Recommended Structure: The Show-Then-Name Carousel

A hybrid of PAS + Personal Journey + Contrarian, designed for Huzz's IP. The framework EMERGES from the story. Never teach first. The audience extracts the lesson, which makes it stickier.

### The 8-Slide Structure

| Slide | Role | What goes here | Tension mechanic |
|-------|------|----------------|------------------|
| **1** | **HOOK** | Contrarian claim or painful truth. No context. No setup. Just the punch. | Open loop: "Wait, what?" |
| **2** | **SCENE** | Drop into a specific moment from your story. Sensory detail. Time + place. "I was standing outside the Sydney Opera House with a pair of headphones and zero people dancing." | Vulnerability creates investment |
| **3** | **THE OLD BELIEF** | What you (and most people) believed before. "I thought I needed another course. Another personality test. More clarity." | Audience sees themselves |
| **4** | **THE CRACK** | The moment the old belief broke. Something happened that made the old story impossible to maintain. "Week 20. I pressed play. Nobody joined. My hands were shaking." | Peak tension. Open loop: what happened? |
| **5** | **THE SHIFT** | What actually changed. Not what you learned intellectually. What you FELT. "Then a stranger put headphones on. Then another. Then 30 people were dancing at the Opera House. My body knew something my mind had been blocking for 13 years." | Emotional payoff |
| **6** | **THE FRAMEWORK** | NOW name the pattern. "I call this the ___." One clean visual: a 2x2, a spectrum, a formula, an equation. The framework earns its name because the story proved it first. | Intellectual payoff after emotional one |
| **7** | **THE MIRROR** | Turn it on the audience. "You have a version of this too. The thing you'd do if fear wasn't running the show." One question that makes them feel seen. | Self-recognition → save/screenshot |
| **8** | **CTA** | Single action. "Save this. Next time fear shows up, check which of the 8 guards is blocking you." Or: "Comment 'dome' and I'll send you the full breakdown." | Bookend: mirror slide 1 visually |

### Why this works

- **Slide 1**: Earns the swipe. Contrarian = curiosity gap.
- **Slide 2**: Story = dwell time. People read stories, they skim tips.
- **Slide 3**: "Old belief" = the audience seeing themselves. They're hooked because it's THEIR belief too.
- **Slide 4**: Peak tension at slide 4 (before the algorithm's mid-point check). Open loop forces the swipe.
- **Slide 5**: Emotional resolution. The payoff that earns the save.
- **Slide 6**: Framework slide. The thing that makes it SHAREABLE (people share frameworks, not feelings).
- **Slide 7**: Mirror. The thing that makes it SAVEABLE (people save what feels personal).
- **Slide 8**: CTA. Clean close.

### Rules

- **Never teach before slide 6.** The framework earns its entrance through the story.
- **One sentence per slide.** Two max. If you need more words, you need another slide.
- **The story must be SPECIFIC.** "I was scared" = skip. "My hands were shaking outside the Opera House at 3pm on a Tuesday" = swipe.
- **The framework slide must be VISUAL.** A 2x2, a numbered list, a formula, a radar chart. Not a paragraph.
- **The mirror question must be a QUESTION.** Not a statement. Questions create internal dialogue. Statements create passive consumption.

## Slide 2 Double Hook Rule (Confirmed by Adam Mosseri)

> "If someone sees your carousel post but they don't swipe, we'll often give that carousel a second chance and automatically move to that second piece of media for the viewer." — Adam Mosseri, Head of Instagram

**Slide 2 IS a second entry point.** It must work as a standalone hook, not a continuation of slide 1.

## Revised Master Structure (v2)

| Slide | Role | Tension technique | Key rule |
|-------|------|-------------------|----------|
| **1** | **HOOK** | Open Loop | Claim that can't stand without proof. One sentence. |
| **2** | **DOUBLE HOOK** | Standalone entry + Breadcrumb | Works WITHOUT slide 1. Different angle on same topic. Plants a breadcrumb. |
| **3** | **STORY OPEN** | Story Pause + Stakes Escalator | Drop into the scene. Specific. Sensory. End mid-tension. |
| **4** | **THE CRACK** | Mid-Sentence Cut or Cliffhanger | Peak tension at The Pit (danger zone). Must force the swipe. |
| **5** | **TEACHING OPEN** | Nested Loop (pause story, open Loop B) | "But to understand why that moment mattered..." Story pauses. Framework enters. |
| **6** | **THE FRAMEWORK** | Progressive Reveal | One clean visual. The saveable/shareable asset. Close Loop B. |
| **7** | **STORY RESOLVE** | Transformation Gap payoff | Return to story. Close Loop A. The emotional + intellectual payoff. |
| **8** | **MIRROR + CTA** | Question Bridge → Save CTA | One question aimed at them + single CTA. Bookend slide 1 visually. |

**Techniques per carousel:** Nested Loop (macro) + 2 micro techniques. Max 3 total.

## CTA Playbook

Ranked by algorithmic value:

1. **Save/Bookmark**: "Save this for the next time you're about to..." (highest for educational carousels)
2. **Share/DM**: "Send this to someone who..." (3-5x algo weight vs likes)
3. **Comment keyword**: "Comment 'dome' and I'll DM you the full breakdown" (lead capture)
4. **Follow**: "Follow @_huzz for one of these every week" (needs posting promise)
5. **Off-Platform**: "Link in bio" (slight algo de-weighting, use sparingly)

**Design rule**: Mirror slide 1's visual style on the CTA slide for a clean bookend.

## Visual Rules

- **4:5 ratio** (1080 x 1350px) for max feed real estate
- Two typeface sizes max (headline + body)
- One accent colour (purple #5e17eb or gold #E9A23B) consistent across all slides
- Same margins/padding every slide
- One idea, one visual per slide
- Progress indicators ("3/8") reduce mid-carousel anxiety
- Mixed format (image + video clip slides) = 29% higher engagement

## Production Workflow

1. Draft the full message as a short article
2. Split into slides with mini-headlines
3. Cut to 6-8 strongest points
4. **Draft hook slide LAST**
5. Add one visual cue per slide
6. Caption should NOT summarise the carousel (defeats swipe incentive)
7. Review save rate at 72 hours
