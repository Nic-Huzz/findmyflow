# Rule Break Tree — Innovation Evolution Framework

Original IP: Huzz Hurrell

## Core Concept

A radial evolutionary tree where every branch point is a **rule break** — someone challenged an inherited assumption and created a new path. The tree visualises how industries evolve through differentiation (splits) and cross-pollination (merges).

- **Splits** = differentiation within an industry (Ford's "any colour as long as it's black" → GM breaks that → colours, sizes, price tiers)
- **Merges** = cross-industry collisions that create new categories (Cars + Mobile = Uber. Cars + Battery = Tesla. Cars + AI = Waymo)
- **New branches** emerge from merges — ride-sharing isn't cars and isn't software, it's born from the collision

## The Centre: Human Experience

**"We are vessels of experience."**

Reviewed by MasterMind Council (Wilber, Watts, Tesla). Verdict: Experience is more philosophically robust than "problems" as the root.

Key arguments:
- You can have experience without naming it as a "problem." You cannot have a "problem" without the experience of something being wrong. Experience is ontologically prior.
- The most revolutionary innovations came from play, curiosity, and desire — not problem-solving. The wheel, fire, music emerged from experience, not articulated problems.
- B2B/infrastructure industries (shipping, semiconductors, agriculture) DO terminate in human experience. A shipping container exists so someone in Norway can taste a mango.
- "Problems" as the centre produces Sisyphean industries that create more problems than they solve. "Experience" as the centre produces industries that expand what's possible.

**Critical rule: Don't impose categories at the root.** Don't label branches "survival / enrichment / wellbeing / convenience" upfront. Let the tree's branching pattern reveal natural clusters. The taxonomy should be discovered, not imposed.

### Starting Point: Hunter-Gatherer Experience

Instead of abstract categories, ground the tree in actual lived experience. A hunter-gatherer's day IS the root system:

- **Movement** → walking → horses → wheels → cars → Uber → Waymo
- **Nourishment** → foraging → agriculture → food processing → restaurants → delivery apps
- **Tools** → stone → metal → machines → computers → AI
- **Status** → body decoration → fashion → cosmetics → luxury goods → branding → social media signalling
- **Bonds** → tribe → village → cities → post → telephone → smartphones
- **Shelter** → caves → huts → architecture → construction → real estate → Airbnb
- **Story** → oral tradition → writing → printing → radio → internet → social media
- **Fire** → warmth/cooking/light → coal → oil → electricity grid → solar → battery tech
- **Healing** → herbs → shamanism → medicine → therapy → wellness apps
- **Threat** → predator scanning → weapons → military → security → surveillance → insurance

10 primal branches. Categories emerge from "what did a human actually DO on a Tuesday 50,000 years ago?" — experienced, not declared.

Note: "Warmth" was renamed to "Fire" because fire was the original multi-tool (warmth, cooking, light, protection, social gathering, tool-making). "Warmth" captured only 20% of what made fire revolutionary. "Threat" and "Status" were added as they spawned major modern industry branches (military/security/insurance and fashion/luxury/branding respectively).

## Rule Break Predictive Formula

```
Rule Break Probability =
    Assumption Age (how long unchallenged)
  × Adjacent Break Density (same assumption already broken in neighbouring industries)
  × Capability Unlock (new tech/method that makes the break POSSIBLE now)
  × Pain Intensity (how much the current rule hurts)
  × Two-Worlds Proximity (someone straddling both sides of the gap)
```

### Why This Is Predictive

- **Assumption Age**: Older, more entrenched assumptions are riper for breaking
- **Adjacent Break Density**: Rule breaks migrate. Subscription pricing broke in software, then razors, then cars, then food. You can watch a break propagate like a wave.
- **Capability Unlock**: New technology makes previously impossible breaks possible. GPS + smartphones + payment APIs → Uber became possible.
- **Pain Intensity**: High pain = high pressure for the break to happen
- **Two-Worlds Proximity**: The person straddling two industries sees assumptions that insiders can't. Uber wasn't a car company innovation — it was tech people looking at taxis.

### Adjacent Possible (Kauffman)

Innovation is constrained to what's adjacent to current capabilities. You can't jump from Model T to Waymo. Every intermediate branch (GPS, computer vision, smartphones, cloud computing) had to exist first. Branches can only grow into adjacent possible space.

## Council Synthesis (Wilber, Watts, Tesla)

### Where They Agreed
Experience is more fundamental than problems. Problems are downstream — they're what you call experience when it encounters resistance. "We are vessels of experience" is philosophically robust and MORE generative because it includes play, awe, curiosity, and desire alongside pain and lack.

### Where They Clashed
- **Wilber**: The centre must be named and operationalised. That's how predictive models work. Use AQAL quadrants to ensure completeness.
- **Watts**: Don't over-define the centre. The Tao that can be named is not the eternal Tao. Let the tree grow and the centre reveals itself.
- **Tesla**: The centre isn't merely "experience" — it's DESIRE. The felt pull toward an experience that doesn't yet exist. Every branch began as someone feeling something the world hadn't yet made possible.

### The Actionable Edge
Use "experience" as the centre. Don't impose categories. Let the branching pattern reveal natural clusters. The taxonomy should be discovered, not imposed.

### Open Question
If humans are vessels of experience, does the vessel itself change shape over time? Are modern humans experiencing DIFFERENTLY than humans 10,000 years ago — not just different things, but different capacities for experience? And if so, does THAT drive innovation too?
