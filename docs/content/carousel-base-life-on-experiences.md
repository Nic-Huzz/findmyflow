# Carousel: Base Your Life on Experiences, Not Achievements

**Pillar:** Experience-based life design
**Audience:** Anyone questioning the default path
**CTA:** General (widest top of funnel)

## Slide 1 (Hook)
Stop planning your career.
Start planning your experiences.

## Slide 2 (The Default)
The default path: pick a job title, work toward it for 10 years, hope happiness follows.

Choose the outcome. Endure the process. Arrive and wonder why it feels empty.

That's an achievement-based life.

## Slide 3 (The Alternative)
The alternative: ask "what experiences make me feel most alive?" then build a life that produces more of them.

Not "what do I want to HAVE?"
But "what do I want to FEEL?"

That's an experience-based life.

## Slide 4 (Why It Works)
We are vessels of experience. That's not a metaphor. It's biology.

Your nervous system doesn't track your job title. It tracks your STATE. Safe or threatened. Alive or numb. Connected or isolated. Expressed or suppressed.

Achievements change your CV. Experiences change your nervous system. One updates your LinkedIn. The other updates your capacity for life.

## Slide 5 (The Evidence)
Research consistently shows:
- Experiences produce more lasting happiness than purchases
- The ANTICIPATION of an experience is itself a source of joy
- Experiences become part of your identity. Objects remain external.
- Shared experiences create bonds that shared possessions never do

You already know this. You've never told a friend about the best spreadsheet you made. You've told them about the sunrise that changed you.

## Slide 6 (The Practical Shift)
Achievement-based decisions:
"Which job pays more?"
"Which city has better career prospects?"
"What skills does the market value?"

Experience-based decisions:
"Which job produces experiences I want MORE of?"
"Which city produces the daily life I feel most alive in?"
"What skills let me access experiences I can't access yet?"

Same decisions. Different filter. Completely different life.

## Slide 7 (The Trap)
"But I need to earn money first, THEN I'll focus on experiences."

This is Head Full of Dreams. Knowing what you want but deferring it behind a condition that never arrives.

The shift isn't "quit your job and travel." It's: "within your current life, what's one experience you keep postponing? Do that this week."

The experience IS the career strategy. Not the reward after.

## Slide 8 (The Examples)
The person who chose "I want to be in the ocean every day" and moved to Bali didn't have a plan. They had a filter.

The person who chose "I want to facilitate transformations" didn't pick a job title. They picked an experience they wanted to produce.

The person who chose "I want to move my body with a tribe" and found CrossFit didn't find a gym. They found an identity.

Every great career decision, in hindsight, was an experience decision disguised as a practical one.

## Slide 9 (The Question)
Write down the 5 experiences that made you feel most alive.

Not achievements. Not milestones. EXPERIENCES.

Now ask: is your current life designed to produce more of those, or fewer?

If fewer — the plan isn't wrong. The FILTER is wrong.

## Slide 10 (CTA)
Your career isn't a ladder. It's a collection of experiences.

The question isn't "what should I do with my life?"
The question is "what experiences do I want my life to be MADE of?"

Start there. Everything else follows.
