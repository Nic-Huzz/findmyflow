# Mismatch Index — Raw Data

Peer-reviewed data collected for the Mismatch Index. Each branch gets an ancestral baseline and a modern average to calculate deviation %.

Formula: `Deviation % = (Baseline - Modern) / Baseline × 100`

| Metric | Ancestral (Hadza) | Modern (US) | Deviation |

| Daily steps | 15,047 | 4,774 | **68.3%** |

**Key insight:** Hadza are 3.15x more active than US adults. But they also have 11.78 hrs/day inactive time — they rest a LOT between movement bouts. The mismatch isn't just "we don't move enough." It's "we don't alternate between movement and rest in the ancestral pattern."

| Metric | Ancestral Baseline | Modern (US) | Deviation |

**Confidence:** HIGH — peer-reviewed survey data with large samples and clear methodology.

- Modern attention: secondary sources only — "8 seconds" claim is NOT reliable

| Metric | Ancestral Baseline | Modern Average | Deviation |

| **Daytime narrative** | 6% of day conversation = storytelling (Wiessner) | Unknown — likely higher (podcasts during commute, etc.) | May actually be CLOSER to baseline during day |

**Key insight:** The deviation here isn't a single number — it's a FORMAT mismatch across multiple dimensions simultaneously: duration (hours → seconds), relationship (known → anonymous), direction (interactive → passive), curation (human → algorithmic). The 81% Wiessner finding is the strongest single data point: evening = story time, and we've replaced campfire stories with doom scrolling.

**Confidence:** Ancestral data = HIGH (Wiessner, PNAS, primary source). Modern comparison = LOW (no clean equivalent metric for "% of evening spent in sustained narrative"). The "8 seconds" attention claim should NOT be used.

**Verified modern data (Edison Research 2024, primary):**

- 23% of those listen 10+ hours/week
- TV: ~32 hours/week (Nielsen, secondary)
- TikTok: ~10.85 min avg session (secondary, not primary-source)
- "8 seconds attention span": NOT VERIFIED — do not use

**The loop-back signal in numbers:** 34% of Americans now consume long-form oral narrative weekly (podcasts). That's 1/3 of the population returning to the campfire format voluntarily. The 23% who listen 10+ hrs/week are spending MORE time in sustained narrative than the Ju/'hoansi evening sessions (~2 hrs/night = ~14 hrs/week). The loop-back audience has EXCEEDED the ancestral baseline in some cases.

## 5. Shelter (CONFIRMED)

**Source studies:**
- Indoor time: EPA/NHAPS (1992-1994, n=340-1,713 per region, 24hr telephone diaries)
- Thermal variation: Brown fat activation study (n=12, 17°C, 2hrs/day, 6 weeks)
- Lighting: Blue-depleted LED vs 4100K fluorescent study (50 lux, 8hrs pre-bedtime)

| Metric | Ancestral Baseline | Modern (US) | Deviation |
|--------|-------------------|-------------|-----------|
| **Time indoors** | ~50% or less (semi-outdoor shelters) | 92.4% (86.9% indoor + 5.5% vehicle) | **~85% more indoor time** |
| **Thermal variation** | Wide daily swings (fire at night, cold mornings, sun midday) | Thermoneutral ~23°C constant | Pattern mismatch — 58% brown fat increase when restored |
| **Evening light** | Firelight (~1,800K, <50 lux, warm red spectrum) | LED/fluorescent (~4,100-6,500K, 100-500 lux, blue-heavy) | Melatonin suppression confirmed |
| **Communal sleeping** | Shared fire, group proximity, co-regulation | Isolated bedrooms, sealed walls | Structural mismatch |

**Key insight:** Shelter has the HIGHEST measurable deviation of any branch (92.4% indoors vs ~50% ancestral). And the metabolic evidence is striking: just 2 hours/day at 17°C for 6 weeks produces 58% more brown fat and 5.2% body fat reduction. The body WANTS thermal variation. We've eliminated it.

**Thermal stability research (2025 scoping review, 29 studies):**
- Indoor temps CAN vary substantially but modern HVAC homes tend toward stability
- More massive/insulated structures = lower temperature swings
- "Safe" thresholds: 18°C-35°C depending on outcome
- Key finding: the literature is starting to ask whether stability ITSELF is desirable — exactly the loop-back question
- Active research area, not settled science

**Confidence:** HIGH for indoor time (EPA primary source). MODERATE for thermal/metabolic effects (small sample n=12, but 2025 scoping review of 29 studies supports the direction). HIGH for lighting/melatonin (controlled study).

## 6. Status (CONFIRMED QUALITATIVELY — no single deviation %)

**Source studies:**
- Hunter-gatherer status: Cross-cultural ethnographic literature, costly signalling theory, Hadza prestige studies
- Modern: Veblen's conspicuous consumption thesis (1899), market research on conscious consumers
- No clean survey series tracking the 10-year shift from consumption-status to skill-status

| Metric | Ancestral Baseline | Modern | Deviation |
|--------|-------------------|--------|-----------|
| **Basis of status** | Earned prestige: skill, generosity, calm leadership, conflict mediation | Consumption display: income, brands, visible wealth markers | Qualitative shift (not a %) |
| **Status plurality** | Multiple paths: hunter, healer, mediator, storyteller all earn status | Converges on wealth/fame/follower count | Narrowing of status pathways |
| **Self-aggrandizement** | Culturally constrained in most forager groups. Overt status-claiming is punished. | Incentivised by platforms. Follower counts, luxury display, personal branding all reward self-promotion. | Pattern inversion |
| **Sharing as status** | Food sharing creates prestige. Generosity > accumulation. | Accumulation creates prestige. Consumption > generosity. | Directional reversal |

**Key insight:** Status deviation isn't measurable as a single % because the METRIC changed. Ancestral: "what you contribute." Modern: "what you own/display." The deviation isn't that we do the same thing worse — it's that we measure status by a completely different signal. The loop-back (de-influencing, skill > stuff, craft revival) is a return to the ancestral metric, not just the ancestral level.

**Loop-back signal:** Younger consumers shifting toward authenticity, expertise, health, sustainability as status markers. But material status hasn't disappeared — it's layered, not replaced.

**Social media impact (Perplexity follow-up):**
The core contrast: **social media rewards broadcastable visibility. Forager societies reward socially validated contribution.**

- Social media turns reputation into a continuous public scoreboard (likes, followers, views)
- Forager status is local, situational, and constrained by anti-boasting norms
- Social media reduces the social cost of self-display and increases the payoff to conspicuous presentation
- Forager norms push competition toward "useful, skilled, admired" — social media pushes toward "visible, countable, displayable"
- Social media doesn't create status competition from nothing — it intensifies an old human motive by making it more public, quantifiable, and always-on

**The deviation in one sentence:** Forager status = "socially validated contribution." Social media status = "broadcastable visibility." The signal decoupled from the substance.

**Deeper framing (Perplexity follow-up):** "Broadcast creates attention. Local reputation creates credibility." In forager groups, these were UNIFIED — everyone saw you contribute AND knew you personally. Modern status SPLIT them: broadcast reach without local trust. The loop-back (creator economy, masterminds, 1000 true fans) RE-COUPLES them — broadcast for reach, local depth for credibility. The deviation isn't broadcast vs local. It's that they decoupled.

**Psychological cost (Perplexity follow-up 2):** The shift from local to broadcast status increases anxiety, chronic social comparison, and self-monitoring. Broadcast status is more VOLATILE (public, quantified, comparative). Local status is more RELATIONAL (grounded in familiarity and trust). The psychological cost = rise in chronic comparison + reputational uncertainty.

**Connection to [[Safety as Mechanism]]:** Broadcast status → chronic comparison → sympathetic activation → can't choose Phase 3 → seek more broadcast validation → deeper in Phase 2. This is the Status-specific version of the Phase 2 trap. The nervous system reads broadcast status as UNSTABLE (volatile, uncontrollable, public) while local status reads as SAFE (familiar, reciprocal, earned). The Phase 3 loop-back (1000 true fans, small communities, skill > stuff) is a return to status conditions the nervous system reads as safe.

**Withdrawal study (Perplexity follow-up 3):** Intentional withdrawal from broadcast platforms CAN restore stability by reducing metricised comparison. But it only "narrows the arena" — it doesn't recreate forager conditions. Local reputation still operates through credentials, taste, and indirect markers. The Phase 3 loop-back for Status is PARTIAL, not complete. Consistent with other branches: loop-back creates a premium segment, not a full reversal.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the qualitative direction (earned prestige → consumption display is well-documented across anthropology and Veblen). LOW for quantification (no single % deviation possible). The conscious consumer market exists but exact size depends on definition.

## 7. Threat (CONFIRMED QUALITATIVELY — like Status, a KIND deviation not DEGREE)

**Source studies:**
- Sapolsky "Why Zebras Don't Get Ulcers" — core framework
- Ancient vs modern stressor perception study — respondents distinguish the two, modern = worse health outcomes
- Human emotional evaluation of ancestral vs modern threats — physical vs abstract/financial/social
- Hair cortisol measurement in contemporary hunter-gatherers
- Pew privacy/control data (partial — specific threat-perception survey data still pending)

| Metric | Ancestral Baseline | Modern | Deviation |
|--------|-------------------|--------|-----------|
| **Stress type** | Acute, concrete, action-oriented (predator, conflict, injury) | Persistent, abstract, uncontrollable (terrorism, climate, economy, social media) | KIND shift (different threat type entirely) |
| **Stress resolution** | Short: threat appears → fight/flee → cortisol clears | Chronic: threat never resolves → cortisol stays elevated → system damage | Pattern inversion |
| **Personal agency** | Direct: you can fight, flee, or hide. The threat is actionable. | Outsourced: the state, corporations, or "the system" handles it. You scroll and worry. | Agency removed |
| **Threat specificity** | Named: the leopard, the rival band, the storm. Known and finite. | Abstract: terrorism, pandemic, data breaches, financial collapse. Unknown and infinite. | From specific to ambient |

**Key insight:** Like Status, the Threat deviation isn't measurable as a single %. The METRIC changed. Ancestral: specific, resolvable, agency-rich. Modern: abstract, chronic, agency-poor. The body's stress response (cortisol spike → clearance) evolved for the first pattern. The second pattern keeps the system activated indefinitely, which is the mechanism behind chronic disease, anxiety disorders, and burnout.

**Sapolsky's core point:** "The body evolved to handle brief emergencies better than prolonged ambiguity." The problem isn't stress. It's stress that never ends.

**Connection to [[Safety as Mechanism]]:** Abstract unresolvable threats → chronic sympathetic activation → can't access ventral vagal → can't choose Phase 3. This is WHY martial arts "work" even when people don't face physical threats — the training COMPLETES the stress cycle the body can't complete against abstract threats. The discharge IS the medicine.

**Biological cost follow-up (Perplexity):** Abstract threats carry HIGHER biological cost because they produce longer, less resolved stress activation. "The body pays not only for danger itself but for the inability to know when it ends." Concrete threats = focused response + shutdown (cheaper). Abstract threats = sustained anticipation without endpoint (chronic). The penalty isn't intensity, it's duration without resolution.

**Confidence:** HIGH for the qualitative direction (acute vs chronic stress is well-documented physiology). HIGH for the biological cost mechanism (anticipatory stress literature supports it). LOW for specific population percentages (survey data on "threats you can't act on" still pending).

## 10. Meta-Theory: Evolutionary Mismatch (ACADEMIC LANDSCAPE)

**Key finding: No universal quantitative Mismatch Index exists in the academic literature.**

Approaches are "relative and domain-specific, not a single standardized score that works across all traits and diseases." This means our cross-domain Mismatch Index IS genuinely novel.

**Key researchers:** Daniel Lieberman (Harvard, exercise/metabolism), Herman Pontzer (Duke, metabolism), Loren Cordain (ancestral diet). Sebastian Junger is popular/social, not core academic.

**Where the theory is strongest:** Physical inactivity, energy-dense diets, sleep/circadian disruption, chronic stress, social isolation. Best when tied to specific exposures + measurable endpoints. (This is exactly what our Index does.)

**Main academic criticisms (we must address these):**
1. **Too elastic** — anything can be called mismatch, making it hard to falsify
2. **Ancestral baseline oversimplified** — no single Pleistocene lifestyle existed
3. **Cherry-picks ancestral evidence** — assumes "natural" = healthy
4. **Underestimates human plasticity** — humans adapt, we're not just running old software

**How our framework differs from academic mismatch:**

| Academic Mismatch | Our Framework |
|---|---|
| Identifies the gap (deviation) | Identifies the gap AND the market response (Phase 3) |
| Domain-specific | Cross-domain (10 branches) |
| No universal index | Mismatch Index with deviation % per branch |
| Descriptive (here's what's wrong) | Predictive (here's what the market will do about it) |
| No mechanism for why people don't change | Safety as Mechanism (NS state determines adoption) |
| No commercial framework | Four Quadrant Scale Test for Phase 3 products |

**Academic verdict:** "Mismatch is best treated as a constrained explanatory framework, not a universal master theory." Our framework goes further by adding the loop-back pattern, Phase 3 theory, and safety mechanism on top of the mismatch concept. We're not contradicting the academic literature — we're extending it with the commercial and behavioral layers it lacks.

**What we must do to be academically defensible:**
1. Always tie claims to specific, measurable exposures (which our Index does)
2. Acknowledge ancestral diversity (not one universal baseline)
3. Not claim the framework explains ALL modern problems
4. Frame it as "constrained explanatory" not "universal master theory"
5. Address the plasticity critique (humans DO adapt, but slower than the branches move)
