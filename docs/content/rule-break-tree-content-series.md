# Rule Break Tree — Content Series

10 standalone pieces + 1 meta piece + 1 video. Each follows the same structure:
**Ancient baseline → Modern deviation → What breaks → The loop-back → CTA**

Hook template: "Your body was designed for X. You're doing Y. Here's what breaks in the gap."

---

## 1. Movement

**Title:** "Your body expects 12,000 steps. You do 4,000."

**Hook:** Hadza hunter-gatherers walk 11-14km/day. The average American does 3,000-4,000 steps. Your hip flexors, glutes, and cardiovascular system were designed for constant low-intensity movement with bursts of sprinting and carrying. You gave them a desk chair for 10 hours.

**The break:** Lower back pain is the #1 global disability cause. 80% of adults experience it. Hip replacements tripled 1990-2010. Obesity went from 15% (1980) to 42% (2020).

**The loop-back:** CrossFit (15K+ boxes), barefoot shoes ($800M market), rucking (400% search spike), cold plunging ($200M+). Every one explicitly framed as "what your body was built for."

**The insight:** The most successful fitness products aren't selling exercise. They're selling a return to what your body expects.

**CTA:** What's YOUR body asking for that you're not giving it?

## 2. Food

**Title:** "Your great-grandmother's diet was better than your nutritionist's"

**Hook:** Hunter-gatherers consumed 100+ plant species seasonally. Your diet relies on 3 (corn, wheat, soy = 60% of calories). They fasted naturally between kills. You eat on a 3-meal schedule invented by factory shift times. They fermented food because it was the only preservation. You eat food designed to never rot.

**The break:** Metabolic syndrome. Obesity doubled in 70+ countries (1980-2010). Ultra-processed food = 60% of American calories, correlating with 90% higher odds of obesity. Type 2 diabetes, ADHD, autoimmune conditions all track the same inflection point.

**The loop-back:** Intermittent fasting ($430M app market). Fermented foods ($255B global). Regenerative agriculture ($13B, growing 18.7% CAGR). Bone broth ($3B+). Every one a return to ancestral eating with modern delivery.

**The insight:** The "new" food movements aren't new. They're 100,000 years old. What's new is the science explaining why they work and the logistics to deliver them at scale.

**CTA:** What would your ancestors recognise on your plate?

## 3. Bonds

**Title:** "338 Facebook friends. Zero people who'd drive you to the airport at 4am."

**Hook:** Your nervous system was designed for a tribe of 150 people who knew you by name. 5 intimate bonds. 15 close ones. Shared meals, shared danger, co-regulation through eye contact and touch. You replaced that with 338 Facebook friends and a loneliness epidemic.

**The break:** US Surgeon General (2023): 50% of adults report measurable loneliness. Lacking social connection = health risk equivalent to smoking 15 cigarettes/day. Teen depression up 66% since 2010. Paradox: more "connected" than ever, lonelier than ever.

**The loop-back:** Men's circles (3x search growth). Co-living ($9B market). Community fitness (CrossFit sells belonging, not fitness). Retreats ($22B). Discord communities cluster below 150 members for high engagement — Dunbar's number, rediscovered digitally.

**The insight:** CrossFit sells belonging, not fitness. Common sells community, not apartments. Evryman sells brotherhood, not therapy. When the marketing language names the ancestral need it restores, that's evidence the mismatch is real.

**CTA:** How many people in your life would you trust with a secret?

## 4. Story

**Title:** "Your brain was built for 2-hour campfire stories. You give it 15-second clips."

**Hook:** 81% of hunter-gatherer evening conversation was storytelling (Wiessner, 2014). Stories activate motor cortex, sensory cortex, and the social brain simultaneously. Your brain evolved for 60-120 minute narrative arcs with a known teller, communal response, and moral resolution. You gave it an infinite scroll of 15-second clips from strangers, curated by an algorithm optimising for outrage.

**The break:** Attention decline correlates r=-0.38 with short-form video use. 50% of TikTok users report videos over one minute feel "stressful." Parasocial relationships replace real bonds. The brain's narrative architecture is being pharmacologically recalibrated downward.

**The loop-back:** Podcasts ($31B → $131B projected). Live events ($1.3T → $3.5T). Substack (4M paid subscribers, 44% open rate vs 22% industry average). Book clubs (350% increase on Eventbrite). Long-form YouTube outperforms Shorts on conversion (2.3% vs 0.8%).

**The insight:** The format that's growing fastest isn't the newest. It's the oldest. Podcasts are campfires. Newsletters are letters from a known voice. Retreats are story circles. The future of media looks like the past of media.

**CTA:** When was the last time you listened to something for two hours without checking your phone?

## 5. Shelter

**Title:** "You spend 90% of your life in a sealed box at 21 degrees"

**Hook:** Humans evolved in semi-outdoor shelters with thermal variation, natural light cycles, communal sleeping, and permeable boundaries. You live in a sealed, climate-controlled box on an isolated lot. 90% of your time is spent indoors breathing recirculated air. Your home is optimised for mortgage payments, not biology.

**The break:** Circadian disruption at population scale. Vitamin D deficiency epidemic. Seasonal affective disorder. The "sick building syndrome" research. Social isolation designed into suburban architecture — no porch, no commons, no ambient contact with neighbours.

**The loop-back:** Biophilic design (living walls, natural light mandates, exposed materials). Tiny homes ($10B market). Co-housing waitlists exceeding supply. "Outdoor rooms" became fastest-growing home renovation category. Passive House standards optimise for biology, not just energy. Circadian lighting systems (warm spectrum at night).

**The insight:** The healthiest home isn't the smartest. It's the one that most closely resembles the conditions your body evolved in: natural light, thermal variation, fresh air, and other humans nearby.

**CTA:** What would change if your home was designed for your biology, not your mortgage?

## 6. Status

**Title:** "Your status used to mean what you can do. Now it means what you own."

**Hook:** In a band of 150 people, you earned status by demonstrated contribution. You tracked, built, healed, mediated, or created — and the tribe watched. Status was skill-visible and locally earned. Three layers of abstraction later: status through consumption (you are what you buy), then status through attention metrics (you are your follower count). Neither requires any demonstrated capability.

**The break:** Identity anxiety. Fast fashion treadmill. Influencer burnout. "Comparison is the thief of joy" as a cultural cliche that doesn't stop anyone from comparing. Status systems three layers removed from anything the brain evolved to process.

**The loop-back:** De-influencing as a major content category. KonMari (30M+ books). "Buy less, buy better" (Patagonia, Veja). Visible mending and repair culture. Craft revival — sourdough, woodworking, pottery. Creator economy shifting from follower count to "1,000 true fans." Skill-visible portfolios.

**The insight:** The highest-status signal in 2026 isn't what you own. It's what you can do. The creator economy is circling back to the original status system: demonstrated competence, witnessed by a known community.

**CTA:** If your follower count disappeared tomorrow, what would you be known for?

## 7. Threat

**Title:** "Your ancestors knew their threats by name. You doomscroll yours."

**Hook:** Your stress response evolved for threats that arrived, peaked, and resolved. The leopard appeared, you fought or fled, cortisol spiked and cleared. Modern threats never resolve. Terrorism, pandemic, financial collapse, climate — permanent ambient danger with no actionable response. Your cortisol spikes and never clears.

**The break:** Chronic stress activation. Anxiety disorders are the most common mental illness globally. The nervous system is stuck in fight/flight because the threats never "end" and you have zero personal agency over them. Outsourced security + abstract threats = helplessness.

**The loop-back:** Martial arts and self-defense (growing steadily, BJJ mainstream). Prepper/resilience communities went mainstream post-COVID. Community emergency preparedness. Personal cybersecurity tools returning agency. Outdoor survival courses selling out.

**The insight:** Martial arts don't work because people face physical threats. They work because training completes the stress cycle the body can't complete against abstract threats. The product isn't safety. It's discharge.

**CTA:** What threat in your life has no resolution? What would it feel like to complete that stress cycle?

## 8. Energy

**Title:** "The $12B product that's actually a 400,000-year-old technology"

**Hook:** For 400,000 years, humans had a direct relationship with energy. You gathered fuel. You built and tended fire. The campfire was the original social technology: where stories were told, food was shared, warmth was communal, and the red-shifted flickering light signalled your body to wind down for sleep. Then we flipped a switch and energy became invisible.

**The break:** Zero relationship with energy source. Blue-light-saturated evenings disrupting circadian biology at population scale. Climate guilt about consumption you have zero agency over. The most complete abstraction of any branch.

**The loop-back:** The candle industry is worth $12 billion globally. Nobody calls it "ancestral fire-light recovery." But that's what it is. Rooftop solar (making your own energy again). Wood-burning stove revival. Campfire culture. Circadian lighting systems. The "energy wellness" product is the one nobody's named yet.

**The insight:** When people light a candle, they're buying a 400,000-year-old technology that their body still recognises as "safety signal, wind down, community." The market acts on the pattern without needing the theory.

**CTA:** When was the last time you sat by a fire and felt something shift?

## 9. Healing

**Title:** "Modern medicine fixed everything except why you feel like shit"

**Hook:** The Healing primal: herbs, touch, ceremony, shamanism. The Medicine branch moved away: surgery, pharmaceuticals, genomics, AI diagnosis. Each step replaced ancient practices with modern science. Life expectancy doubled. Acute disease conquered. But chronic disease, burnout, anxiety, depression, and "feeling like shit despite being objectively fine" — these are AT ALL TIME HIGHS.

**The break:** Medicine optimised for fixing what's broken, not maintaining what works. Chronic disease now accounts for 90% of US healthcare spending. Mental health crisis. Burnout as a medical diagnosis (WHO, 2019). The vessel is being repaired after it breaks instead of maintained before.

**The loop-back:** Breathwork renaissance ($200M+ market). Psychedelic therapy (MDMA, psilocybin in clinical trials). Cold exposure. Somatic therapy. Functional medicine. Wellness retreats ($22B). Every one revives an ancient practice with modern understanding layered on (polyvagal theory, nervous system science, microbiome research).

**The insight:** Wellness isn't an alternative to medicine. It's the missing PHASE. Medicine is emergency repair. Wellness is ongoing maintenance. Your ancestors didn't choose between the two. They did both. We dropped the maintenance and wondered why everything keeps breaking.

**CTA:** What if the thing your body needs isn't a new treatment, but an ancient practice you stopped doing?

## 10. The Meta Piece

**Title:** "Every industry follows the same three phases. Most people are stuck in Phase 2."

**Hook:** We studied 10 industries across 600 years. Every single one follows the same pattern: build outward (make things faster, cheaper, more convenient), peak (the convenience starts hurting more than helping), loop back (return to what our bodies were designed for, delivered through modern tools).

**The three phases:**
1. Forward: innovate, scale, remove stressors
2. Deviation peak: mismatch with 100,000-year-old biology becomes painful
3. Loop-back: return to primal conditions via capacity building

Phase 3 isn't a trend. It's a PHASE that every industry enters when the gap between modern convenience and ancient biology gets too wide. And the $12B candle industry proves people are already buying it without anyone naming the pattern.

**The insight:** Wellness isn't an industry. It's vessel maintenance. We are vessels of experience, and when the vessel breaks down from the mismatch, Phase 3 products repair it. The future of every industry looks like the past of human experience, delivered at modern scale.
