# Founder Journey Discovery Session

**Date:** January 28, 2025
**Purpose:** Ensure FindMyFlow captures the essential elements of Nic's 5-year journey, compressed into 5 weeks to 1 year for users.

---

## Part 1: The Starting Point

### Q1: What was the actual trigger 5 years ago?

Final year of university, I had a casual job at an adventure capital firm and festival as part of the programs team. I thought this was my dream job because it merged two of my passions: education and business.

At the end of university, they offered me a full-time job. However, a month into starting, I didn't find happiness, fulfillment, or joy—any of the things I thought were promised from following the traditional path of school → university → internships → full-time job.

I found myself having an existential crisis. The best way I can describe it: **it was like an earthquake hit my understanding of how the world worked.** I followed this path, this process, because I trusted the goal was happiness, fulfillment, joy. But when I didn't find any of these things, it was like an earthquake hit every building of understanding that I had and made it crumble.

For the past five years I've been rebuilding each building about how it actually works. At that time in my life I was just left with questions like:
- Who am I?
- What's my purpose?
- What am I doing with my life?
- Why is everyone walking this path and living this way if it actually doesn't bring the thing I thought was promised?

---

### Q2: What did you think you needed vs. what you actually needed?

**What I thought I needed:** More knowledge.

After university I ended up doing another **42 different learning experiences** and spending another **$30,000** thinking that each course was the answer for finding the way out and living life with happiness and joy.

After three years and all this knowledge accumulation, I found myself in the exact same place—fed up.

**What I actually needed:** To start taking action—action that terrified me.

The terrifying factor was vulnerability. I feared judgment. I feared not being good enough. I feared failure.

I challenged myself to **do one thing a week that absolutely terrified me for a whole year**:
- Within 4 weeks: Working from Bali
- Within 3 months: Quit my job
- Within 5 months: Funding my life traveling Thailand and Bali

**Key insight:** We don't rise to the level of our ambitions. We fall to the level of what we feel safe doing. I needed to expand what my nervous system felt safe doing—to remove the resistance, the tension, the fear that was stopping me from taking action.

## Part 2: The Journey

### Q3: What were the 3-5 most significant breakthroughs?

1. **The Knowledge Trap Realization**
   - Paid for 42 programs/learning experiences but nothing about my physical reality changed
   - Made me realize the answer wasn't more knowledge

2. **The Fear Challenge Breakthrough**
   - Getting outside my comfort zone weekly
   - How quickly that led my environment to change

3. **The Flow Philosophy**
   - Belief that there's a path out there that only you can walk
   - The universe communicates what that path is every day through **ease and resistance**
   - **Ease:** Serendipity, things just happening, flowing quickly, progress happening fast
   - **Resistance:** No matter what you do or try, nothing goes to plan
   - **Key insight:** Resistance is the universe's way of saying there's a better path. Resistance is actually a positive redirection.

---

### Q4: What nearly made you quit?

The biggest struggle after quitting and finding my flow was **financial security**.

I call it the **"runway game"**—the amount of savings I was willing to spend to figure it out. You can multiply that runway by number of weeks to figure out how long you have (like a startup with funding runway).

The challenge: Experience and progress didn't happen as quickly as I thought it would.

---

### Q5: What took the longest to figure out that now seems obvious?

1. **Transformation doesn't happen from knowledge acquisition—it happens from taking action.**

2. **The end result is rarely what you planned.** The importance is:
   - Letting go of expectation
   - Not anchoring to an end result
   - Just knowing what the next step is
   - Listening to feedback
   - Making it 3% better every time you deliver something

3. **Fear of judgment is entirely mental—it's a projection.**
   - Once you expect the best (not the worst) from a situation, anxiety turns into excitement
   - Or at minimum, nervousness instead of paralyzing fear

## Part 4: Deep Dive - The Paralysis Phase

### Q: How long were you in the "who am I?" phase?

About **18 months** feeling really lost and isolated. It didn't help that it coincided with lockdowns of 2020-2021.

### Q: What got you out of paralysis?

**Online courses where I could show up as the new person I was** and be seen and accepted as that person.

For a long time with existing friendships, I became a **bad version of my new-self and a bad imposter of my old-self** because I didn't feel safe showing up and being seen as this new person. It felt too vulnerable.

Then the big breakthrough was the **fear challenge** where I had no choice but to be seen by everyone.

## Part 5: Deep Dive - The Identity Framework

### Q: How did you pick what terrifying thing to do each week?

It was **intuitive based on what scared me**—specifically vulnerability, which means owning your identities.

### The Vulnerability-Fear-Identity Framework:

**Why are vulnerable things scary?**
- That vulnerable thing brings great joy
- To open yourself up to judgment creates opportunity for pain
- Something you don't want to feel
- Because you want to protect this identity and keep it as something joyful

**Why we resist things we don't like:**
- You don't want to be given that identity as it doesn't align with who you see yourself as

**Why we resist things we DO identify with:**
- You don't want that identity to be broken
- Example: Dream of being a great stand-up comedian → Actually doing stand-up and being bad at it breaks that identity → Terrifying
- **Solution: Need to identify with the process, not the outcome**

**The Fear Hack:**
- I didn't fear the magic show or stand-up comedy because my identity was attached to "I do scary things"
- To NOT do the scary things would break that identity
- The fear/pain of NOT doing scary things became worse than the pain/fear of DOING the scary thing

**Why owning your vulnerabilities removes the fear:**
- The fear of vulnerability is the fear of potential pain
- Once you own it, you prove to yourself it's not scary
- By owning it, you gain a tribe of people who identify you as that and reassure you
- Now what random people think isn't as important
- Before claiming the identity, there's equal evidence for both versions (both are 0), which hurts
- **One purpose of the game: owning your vulnerability**

## Part 6: Deep Dive - Resistance vs. Redirection

### Q: How do you know the difference between "this is redirection, pivot" vs. "this is just hard, push through"?

Based on the **emotional response to the challenge**:
- If it's a challenge you **enjoy** → That's a growth edge to push against
- If it's something you **don't enjoy** or things keep going wrong/not to plan → That's a redirection

### 1. "Identify with Process, Not Outcome" Teaching

**The problem:** People attach identity to being a "great comedian" instead of "someone who practices comedy." When they're bad, the identity shatters.

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Process Identity Selection** | During onboarding or Groan intro, users choose WHO they are in the journey: "Explorer," "Experimenter," "Learner" — not what they want to achieve | Onboarding / Groan Matrix intro |
| **Pre-challenge reframe** | Before each Groan, show: "You're not trying to be good at X. You're being someone who tries X." | Groan challenge modal |
| **The Comedian Story** | Nic's actual example as a 60-second teaching moment — powerful because it's concrete | First Groan onboarding or Healing tab |
| **Outcome vs. Process toggle** | When users set a challenge, ask: "What's the outcome you want?" then reframe: "Your real goal: Be someone who [process version]" | Challenge creation flow |

#### Recommendation
The **pre-challenge reframe** is low-effort, high-impact. Every time someone starts a Groan, they see the identity they're building is "someone who does this" not "someone who's good at this."

---

### 2. Meta-Identity Progression ("I Do Scary Things")

**The problem:** Individual challenges are disconnected. The magic happened when Nic's identity became "I do scary things" — making NOT doing them painful.

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Identity evolution badges** | Visible progression: "First Step" → "Edge Toucher" → "Fear Facer" → "Scary Thing Doer" — with ceremony at each level | Profile / Groan Matrix |
| **Streak as identity** | "You've shown up for 12 scary things. You're someone who does this now." Breaking streak = breaking identity | Challenge tracking |
| **Identity declaration ritual** | After X completions, prompt: "You've proven it. Say it: 'I do scary things.'" — make them type/speak it | Milestone moment |
| **Reversal framing** | After enough completions, challenges frame as: "Not doing this would break who you've become" instead of "Do this scary thing" | Challenge prompts |
| **Public identity claim** | Option to share "I'm someone who does scary things" to their network — claiming it externally | Social sharing |

#### Recommendation
**Streak as identity + identity declaration ritual**. The streak makes NOT doing it costly. The ritual makes them consciously claim it.

### 3. Relationship Navigation During Transformation

**The problem:** Users transform in the app, then regress around old friends/family who knew the "old them."

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **The Purgatory Acknowledgment** | Simple card: "You might feel like a bad version of your new self AND a bad imposter of your old self around people who knew you before. This is normal." | Early in journey, after first few Groans |
| **Relationship audit prompt** | Reflection: "Who in your life supports this new version? Who pulls you back to the old version?" — not to cut people off, but awareness | Healing tab or periodic check-in |
| **Scripts/conversation starters** | "Here's how to explain your journey to people who knew the old you" — reduces friction of being seen | Tools or downloadable resource |
| **Pre-visit preparation** | Before holidays/family visits, a prompt: "You're about to see people who knew the old you. Remember who you're becoming." | Triggered by calendar or manual |
| **Group as anchor** | Explicit framing: "This group sees the real you. When old relationships feel hard, come back here." | Community messaging |

#### Recommendation
**The Purgatory Acknowledgment** is essential — just naming it reduces shame. Could be a one-time card that appears after 2-3 weeks in the app.

### 4. Serendipity Design in Groan Matrix

**The problem:** Too stage-driven. Nic's breakthroughs (silent discos, headset rentals) weren't planned — they flowed.

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Wild card challenges** | Random, unexpected prompts: "Today, say yes to something you'd normally say no to" — not tied to stages | Groan Matrix or daily prompt |
| **"What flowed today?" capture** | End of day/week prompt: "Anything unexpected happen? Capture it." — tracks serendipity retroactively | Daily check-in |
| **Open-ended Groans** | Instead of specific challenges, some are: "Do something that scares you today. You decide what." | Groan Matrix |
| **Community cross-pollination** | "Someone in your group did X. Would you try something similar?" — organic inspiration | Group activity feed |
| **Reduce stage gates** | Allow jumping between stages based on energy/interest, not rigid progression | Stage system flexibility |
| **"Flow opportunity" alerts** | Based on location, time, weather: "It's sunny. A beach silent disco happened on a day like this..." | Push notification |

#### Recommendation
**"What flowed today?" capture + open-ended Groans**. The first catches serendipity that already happened. The second creates space for it.

### 5. "Grief of the Traditional Path" Processing

**The problem:** The earthquake moment — realizing "I was lied to" — needs processing, not just moving forward.

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Earthquake Inventory** | Flow that asks: "What beliefs crumbled? What were you promised? What did you actually get?" | Healing tab, early in journey |
| **Letter to the system** | Write to "the path," "the promise," or your past self — process the anger/grief | Healing exercise |
| **"You're not alone" stories** | Others' earthquake moments — normalizes the experience | Community or curated content |
| **Rebuild inventory** | After processing: "What new beliefs are you building?" — moves from grief to construction | Follows Earthquake Inventory |

#### Recommendation
**Earthquake Inventory** as an optional Healing flow. Not forced, but available for those in that phase.

---

### 6. Runway/Income Bridge Calculator

**The problem:** Financial security was the biggest wall. Users need to see how long they have and plan accordingly.

#### Implementation Options

| Approach | How it works | Where it lives |
|----------|--------------|----------------|
| **Runway Calculator** | Input savings, monthly expenses → shows weeks/months of runway | Tools / Stage 8 |
| **Income Bridge Tracker** | Track side income growth vs. job income → show progress toward replacement | Dashboard widget |
| **"Stay employed" mode** | Explicitly frame the journey as "build while employed" with milestones for when to consider transition | Onboarding / Settings |
| **Weekly income goal** | Based on runway, calculate: "You need to generate $X/week by [date] to extend runway" | Weekly planning |

#### Recommendation
**Runway Calculator + Income Bridge Tracker** as complementary tools. One shows the countdown, the other shows progress toward safety.
