## The Ship30 Formula

Their entire email engine runs on a **repeating 3-phase cycle**:

```
NURTURE (value emails, 2-3x/week)
    -> WARM-UP (waitlist + educational series, 5-14 days depending on product)
        -> LAUNCH (cart open + proof + objection + urgency + close, 7-10 days)
            -> back to NURTURE
```

**Key metrics from their inbox:**
- 65 emails in ~65 days = almost daily during launches, 2-3x/week during nurture
- 3 product launches in 2 months (Low-Ticket Launchpad, Self-Publishing Studio, Start Writing Online Sprint)
- The structure is repeated for every launch, but the warm-up length and format vary by product price/complexity
- "Re:" resend on every major email (waitlist, pre-launch, launch day) to the FULL list
- Emails sent from varying sender identities (see Sender Identity Rotation below)

### Sender Identity Rotation

Ship30 rotates the "From" name to match the product or tone:

| Sender Name | Used For |
|-------------|----------|
| "Dickie & Cole" | Core brand, launches, joint announcements |
| "Nicolas Cole" | Personal stories, solo value emails |
| "Cole at AI Writing Skool" | AIWS membership content, hot seat promos |
| "Start Writing Online" | Sprint-specific launch sequence |
| "Dickie at Ship 30 for 30" | Ship30-specific cohort promos |

**Your equivalent:** Most emails from "Huzz" or "Huzz at Vibe Rise." For specific products, vary: "Huzz at Shift Architecture" (certification), "Start Running Experiences" (sprint product).

## Launch Anatomy: All 3 Launches Compared

The core structure is consistent but the warm-up phase adapts to each product's price point and audience readiness.

### Launch 1: Low-Ticket Launchpad LIVE ($350, Apr 13-27)

The "textbook" launch. 4-pillar educational warm-up, full 14-day cycle.

```
Day -7:  WAITLIST       -- Announce upcoming thing, join waitlist
Day -7:  RESEND (Re:)   -- Same email, evening (shorter TL;DR version)
Day -6:  PILLAR #1      -- "Digital Builder Pillar #1: Product Creation"
Day -5:  PILLAR #2      -- "Digital Builder Pillar #2: Offer & Pricing"
Day -4:  PILLAR #3      -- "Digital Builder Pillar #3: Tech Stack"
Day -3:  PILLAR #4      -- "Digital Builder Pillar #4: Marketing"
Day -2:  SOCIAL PROOF   -- Case study compilation email
Day -1:  PRE-LAUNCH     -- "[Tomorrow] Doors open..."
Day -1:  RESEND (Re:)   -- Evening, shorter version
Day  0:  LAUNCH         -- Full sales email (~18K chars, long)
Day  0:  RESEND (Re:)   -- Evening, shorter TL;DR
Day +1:  BENEFIT RECAP  -- "Invest once, sell forever" + "response has been amazing"
Day +2:  CASE STUDY     -- Specific numbers: "How a beginner writer made $5M"
Day +3:  OBJECTION      -- Emotional: "A message from future-you" / "Where will you be?"
Day +4:  CASE STUDY     -- Another case study with specific numbers
Day +4:  VALUE          -- Contrarian value piece (same day, different angle)
Day +5:  VALUE STACK    -- Blueprint/value stack reminder: "Want our blueprint?"
Day +6:  PRE-CLOSE      -- "Starts tomorrow" (rocket emojis, excitement)
Day +6:  URGENCY        -- "Please don't email us tomorrow..." (classic)
```

### Launch 2: Self-Publishing Studio ($800, May 17-31)

Higher price point = LONGER warm-up. Instead of a numbered "4 pillars" series, they ran **7 consecutive days of book-themed value content** before pre-launch. Each email taught a standalone lesson while building desire for the paid program.

```
WARM-UP (7 days of daily topic emails):
Day -9:  "Why write a book" (identity/authority framing)
Day -8:  "Feeling overwhelmed?" (empathy + framework preview)
Day -7:  "How to outline 60,000 words" (tactical, builds confidence)
Day -6:  "You, published author" (identity projection, future self)
Day -5:  "Writing a book is like dating" (unexpected analogy, memorable)
Day -4:  Hormozi marketing secret (borrowed authority)
Day -3:  Blog post -> best-seller framework (Ferriss, Gladwell, Holiday)

PRE-LAUNCH + LAUNCH (same structure as Launch 1):
Day -2:  SOCIAL PROOF compilation
Day -1:  PRE-LAUNCH "[Tomorrow]" + RESEND
Day  0:  LAUNCH DAY + RESEND
Days 1-6: Same open-cart structure (proof, objection, urgency, close)
```

**Key difference:** The warm-up for a higher-priced product isn't more emails per day. It's more days of warm-up. Each daily email is standalone educational content that builds desire for the paid thing.

### Launch 3: Start Writing Online Sprint (~$97, Jun 7-14)

Lowest price = SHORTEST warm-up. Almost no educational pre-launch. Just 2 days (a waitlist closing notice + an AIWS event promo), then straight into launch + 4 days of open cart.

```
Day -2:  "Waitlist closing" (urgency on the pre-list)
Day -1:  AIWS Hot Seat event promo (engagement anchor)
Day  0:  LAUNCH DAY + RESEND
Days 1-4: Abbreviated open cart (proof, objection, close)
```

**Key insight:** For low-ticket offers, the warm-up is minimal because the price doesn't require as much trust-building. The audience has already been nurtured by ongoing value emails.

### Warm-Up Length by Price Point

| Product | Price | Warm-Up Days | Warm-Up Format |
|---------|-------|-------------|----------------|
| Start Writing Online Sprint | ~$97 | 2 days | Minimal (waitlist + event) |
| Low-Ticket Launchpad LIVE | $350 | 7 days | 4-pillar educational series |
| Self-Publishing Studio | $800 | 9 days | 7 daily topic emails |
| Ship 30 for 30 (cohort) | ~$500 | Not in dataset | Likely 5-7 days |

**Rule of thumb:** ~1 day of warm-up per $50-100 of price.

## The "Re:" Resend (Clarified)

**Correction from previous analysis:** The "Re:" emails are NOT sent only to non-openers. They go to the **full list** as shortened follow-up versions.

Evidence from the email bodies themselves:
- "Just wanted to follow-up real quick to make sure you didn't miss our email from this morning"
- "Here's the TL;DR."

**What the Re: emails actually do:**
1. Shorter than the original (summary/TL;DR version, not a full copy-paste)
2. Sent 6-8 hours after the original (morning send, evening Re:)
3. Create the illusion of a personal conversation thread ("Re:" in subject line)
4. Give people who DID open the first email a second, more concise touchpoint
5. Catch people who missed the first email in their inbox

**When they use it:** Every critical email. Waitlist open, pre-launch "[Tomorrow]", Launch Day. Never on regular value emails.

**Your implementation:** Set up a 6-8hr delayed send for key emails. Write the Re: version as a genuine short follow-up, not an identical copy. Include a one-line TL;DR of the main email and a direct CTA.

## Companion Content Engine

This is the critical finding: **Ship30's emails are not standalone. They reference and drive traffic to a constellation of companion content.** The emails are the distribution layer, not the content itself.

### YouTube Videos (Referenced in 16 of 65 emails)

Videos are created independently and then referenced in emails as value adds or proof points.

| Video Topic (Ship30) | Email Context |
|----------------------|---------------|
| "How to make your product's offer 10x better" | Linked from value email as "watch this" |
| "How to grow on LinkedIn" | Value email wrapping the video topic |
| "3 Steps to Build Your First Claude Skill" | AI-themed value email with video embed |
| "How to create paid courses students LOVE" | Pre-launch warm-up, builds desire |
| "$500k+ from book royalties" | Social proof, linked during book launch |

**Pattern:** ~1 YouTube video per week. The email either recaps the video's key points or teases it and drives clicks. Some emails are entirely "new video dropped, here's why you should watch."

### AIWS Hot Seat (Weekly Live Sessions)

Referenced throughout the 65 emails as an ongoing engagement anchor. Their equivalent of a weekly office hours / coaching call.

- Mentioned in event promo emails ("About tomorrow's AIWS Hot Seat")
- Used as a CTA in value emails ("Bring your questions to this week's Hot Seat")
- Creates recurring touchpoint independent of launches

### Free Resources and Mini-Courses

| Resource | How It's Used |
|----------|--------------|
| FREE Newsletter Mini-Course | "Build your newsletter landing page in 90 minutes." Standalone lead magnet email. |
| Writer Career Paths eBook | Lead magnet, referenced in nurture sequence |
| 30 Writing Prompts | Free resource drop, drives engagement |
| Book Monetization Mastery ($997 value) | Time-limited bonus stacked onto Self-Publishing Studio launch |

### "3 Best Posts from the Week" Recap Format

A recurring email format (roughly weekly during nurture phases) that:
1. Recaps 3 social media posts from that week
2. Includes a 1-2 line summary of each post's key insight
3. Links to the original posts (LinkedIn, Twitter/X)
4. Requires zero original writing (just curation of existing content)

**Why this matters:** It turns your social media content into email content for free. One week of posting = one email written in 10 minutes.

## Key Copywriting Patterns

### Subject Line Formulas

- **[BRACKET] + Announcement**: "[WAITLIST OPEN]", "[LAUNCH DAY]", "[last call]", "[Tomorrow]"
- **Specific numbers**: "How a beginner writer made $5,000,000"
- **Lowercase casual**: "why write a book", "still want to write online?"
- **Question format**: "Am I even qualified to write a book?"
- **Curiosity gap**: "Please don't email us tomorrow..."
- **"You" framing**: "A message from future-you", "[your name], published author"
- **"Re:" prefix**: On all key emails for the resend
- **Video indicator**: Include "New video:" or reference to signal multimedia

### Email Structure Patterns

1. **Pillar emails**: "Yesterday we explained X. Today: Pillar #N." Teaching content, one mini case study, "more tomorrow" hook, CTA to waitlist.
2. **Social proof**: Open with a number or result. Tell the story. "Here's what they did differently." Link to product.
3. **Objection emails**: State the fear verbatim. Empathize. Reframe. "What if..." then CTA.
