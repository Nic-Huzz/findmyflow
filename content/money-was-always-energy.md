## Format 2: Long-form Essay (Newsletter/Blog)

### Money Was Always Energy (And AI Just Made That Obvious)

A dollar is a battery.

That sounds weird, so let me explain.

Before money existed, humans traded energy directly. I spent 4 hours hunting a deer, you spent 4 hours gathering berries, we swapped. The "price" of the deer was the energy I burned catching it. Simple.

The problem was you had to be there. If I hunted a deer on Tuesday but you didn't have berries until Friday, we were stuck. So we invented a token. A coin. A receipt that said "this person spent X amount of energy creating something valuable."

Money was never its own thing. It was always a proxy for human energy expenditure. A dollar represents "someone spent energy to create this value."

That's all it ever was.

**The drift**

The chain from there is pretty straightforward once you see it.

Coins worked because they were hard to fake and everyone agreed they had value. But the real innovation wasn't the metal. It was the trust. I don't know you, you don't know me, but we both trust the coin. Money was a trust technology. It let strangers cooperate without being related, without being friends, without even speaking the same language.

Then banks showed up and things got interesting. They let you trade coins you didn't have yet. Credit. Suddenly you could spend your future energy today. The token was no longer just a receipt for past effort. It was a claim on effort that hadn't happened.

Then governments cut the cord entirely. Fiat money. The US left the gold standard in 1971 and the token stopped being backed by anything physical at all. It was backed by belief. By trust in the government. The receipt for human energy became a receipt for collective agreement.

This is where most people live today. They earn money, spend money, worry about money, chase money, and never once think about the fact that the entire system is a 10,000-year-old workaround for the problem of "I can't be physically present every time my energy creates value."

**Bitcoin reconnected the wire**

I think most people misunderstand what Bitcoin actually did.

The popular narrative is that Bitcoin's innovation was decentralisation. Taking power from banks, removing the middleman, all that. And that's true. But it's not the deeper thing.

The deeper thing is that Bitcoin made the money-energy link explicit again. Proof of work means you literally convert electricity into coins. A Bitcoin IS stored energy. Not metaphorically. Physically. You can calculate exactly how many kilowatt-hours went into mining it.

For the first time since barter, money was directly, measurably backed by energy. Not by trust in a government. Not by the weight of a gold bar. By electricity.

Frederick Soddy, who won the Nobel Prize in Chemistry in 1921, wrote about this almost a century ago. His argument was that real wealth is energy transforming materials. Everything humans call "wealth" is just energy that's been applied to raw stuff. Money, he said, is artificial accounting. A useful fiction.

Buckminster Fuller said the same thing in 1967: "Our new economic basis wouldn't be gold or dollars. It would be kilowatt-hours."

And Musk, on the Nikhil Kamath podcast in December 2025: "Energy is the true currency."

Three people, a century apart, all pointing at the same thing. Money is energy with extra steps.

**AI removes the steps**

Here's where it gets interesting.

Money was the abstraction layer between human energy and value. You spent an hour of YOUR energy, and the dollar let you store and transfer that energy without being there. The dollar was the middleman between your body and the thing your body made.

AI changes this in a way nothing else has.

When an AI powered by solar panels designs a product, writes the marketing copy, handles customer service, and processes the payment, value got created. Real, exchangeable, useful value. But no human spent energy creating it. The energy came from the sun, through a panel, into a server, through a model.

Energy in, value out. No human labour required.

So what does the dollar measure?

It used to measure "someone spent their energy making this." Now the energy making things isn't someone's. It's the grid's. It's the sun's. It's a nuclear reactor's.

Money was the proxy between human energy and value. When AI replaces human energy, the proxy has nothing to abstract. It's like a translator in a room where everyone already speaks the same language.

This is what Musk means when he says "things won't be measured in dollars then, just mass and energy." He's not being utopian. He's being literal. When AI and robotics can satisfy any human need using energy, the only question that matters is "do you have the energy?" Not "do you have the money?"

**Phase 3 Reversion**

I've been mapping what I call the Rule Break Tree. It's a framework for how every domain of human experience evolves through the same three phases. Phase 1 is the original break. Phase 2 is optimisation, where insiders refine the break until it drifts far from the original experience. Phase 3 is the snap-back, where someone uses modern capability to recover what was natural before the drift.

Money drifting from energy is a textbook Phase 2 story. Each step (coins, paper, fiat) made money more abstract, more convenient, more scalable. But each step also took it further from what it actually was. Stored human energy.

What AI is doing looks like Phase 3. A reversion. Not back to barter, but forward to something that reconnects value creation to energy directly. The abstraction layer between effort and output gets thinner and thinner until it disappears.

The $50 you earn per hour is a proxy for one hour of your energy applied to a problem. When AI applies energy to that same problem without needing your hour, the $50 stops making sense. Not because money is evil or broken. Because the thing it was designed to measure, human energy expenditure, is no longer the bottleneck.

**What this means for you and me**

I'm not writing this to say money is disappearing tomorrow. It's not. The transition will be messy, slow, political, and probably violent in places.

But the direction is clear. And if you're someone who's building a career, a business, or a life right now, the question worth sitting with is: what am I actually trading?

If the answer is "my time for money," you're trading your energy through a 10,000-year-old abstraction layer that's about to get a lot thinner.

If the answer is "my unique perspective, my relationships, my ability to create experiences that make people feel something," you're trading something AI can't replace. Not because AI isn't smart enough. Because those things aren't energy problems. They're human problems.

Money was always energy in disguise. The disguise is coming off.
